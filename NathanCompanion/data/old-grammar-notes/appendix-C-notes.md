# Old Irish notes: summaries, course notes, exercises and miscellaneous files

Scope: the non-verb, non-declension, non-dictionary files in `Grammar/` (plain text in `txt/`). Read-only analysis; nothing in the app repo was changed.

## Headline findings

- **The best thing in this batch is "Deismir maith.doc"**, which is not the teacher's own notes. It is the full text (about 66,000 words) of *Deismir: cnuasach de chora cainte is de natháin i nGaeilge Uladh*, compiled by **Anraí Mac Giolla Chomhaill** (An Lúb, Co. Dhoire), dated September 2002 and published by the **Armagh Diocesan Historical Society**. The document's internal title is "A Dictionary of Ulster Irish Expressions". It is an English-to-Irish dictionary of Ulster idioms, laid out like Clyne's Gairm dictionary of Gaelic expressions: about 3,100 English headwords, each with example sentences. It is a genuine Ulster (Donegal-flavoured) source and ideal for lifting Comhrá answers. It is also **a copyrighted published book**, so the app can only use a small, curated, attributed selection unless permission is obtained.
- **The two "Nótaí ... Iúil 2003" files and "Dioplóma 1/2, 2003" belong to the same course.** They are the tutor's handbook and class notes for the **NUI Galway Dioplóma sa Ghaeilge** (author metadata "Máire Uí Ghriallais"; exam papers headed "Ollscoil na hÉireann, Gaillimh"), apparently delivered in Belfast (pupils give "a s(h)eoladh i mBéal Feirste"). They are course-provider material, adult-level, Connacht in dialect and Republic-centred. They are rich in **ideas** (discussion questions, opinion phrase banks, agree/disagree statements, review phrases, problem-page scenarios, error-correction passages) but the text should not be reused verbatim.
- **Colleagues wrote much of the Armagh-specific material.** "Gerard/Gerald McKeown" (C2k: gmckeown862), who is also "Gearóid Mac Eoin" of the MA translation folder, wrote Logainmneacha Ard Mhacha, Bailte Móra agus Contaetha, UIMHREACHA and AS A2 Léamh; "godochartaigh" wrote the IMU adjective pack and the blank county map. It is good Ulster work with small errors, and it can be used once the colleagues agree.
- **The rulings are breached in several places, mainly in model sentences:** "dún" (Gramadach na Gaeilge 1 and 2 use Dún as the model first-conjugation verb), "clois" (diploma grammar notes, Gramadach 1 and 2), "-amar" (Gramadach 1 "chonaiceamar"; AS A2 Léamh "rabhamar"), and "bord" (the diploma notes use bord as the example noun for every preposition). None of it can be imported without rewriting.
- **Gaps in the app that this material fills:** the Year 8 builders have no days of the week (no "Dé Luain" anywhere in `units.json`), no full dates, and no county or town names beyond the local villages. The Comhrá builders contain none of the idioms or opinion phrases below.

## 2. Interrogation

Not a ruling but worth noting: synthetic first-person plurals (*cuirimid, táimid, dhúnfaimis, bhrisfimis*) appear in AS A2 Léamh and Gramadach 2. Ulster usage is *cuireann muid, tá muid, dhruidfeadh muid*.

### 2.2 Non-Ulster forms (dialect)

- **Connacht (diploma files):** *Céard* for *Cad é*; *Táim, Nílim* for *Tá mé, Níl mé*; the urú after preposition plus article (*ag an gcistin, ar an gcapall, leis an bpobal, faoin bpost, an bpá*); *breathnaigh* for *amharc*; *go hiondúil*. The diploma grammar notes teach "ag + an = ag an + urú: ag an gcistin", which contradicts the app's lesson "Réamhfhocal + an: séimhiú in Ulaidh".
- **Munster (Achoimre, An Séimhiú):** *dein, tigh, fé, sinn, tosnaíonn, tré, daoibh, don bhfear, sa tigh*. The Achoimre states the urú rule as the default ("Réamhfhocal + an + urú de ghnáth ... ar an mbóthar") and so also contradicts the app.
- **Ulster, used correctly:** Gramadach 1, Míreanna ceisteacha, the IMU pack, Bailte Móra, the MA translations and Deismir.
- **Older or Donegal-only forms in Deismir:** *cha/chan* negatives, *thig, bhéarfaidh, gheibhthear, dhéanfas, tiocfas*, and the relative *-(e)as*. They are authentic Ulster but not the register pupils should produce. Any selection should use the modern Ulster written form (for example, *níl* rather than *chan fhuil*) unless the idiom depends on the older form.

### 2.3 Errors, file by file (examples, not exhaustive)

- **Nótaí Comhrá:** "Uaiteanta solúbtha oibre" (uaireanta); "Teagmháil leis na bpobal" (leis an phobal); "daone" (daoine); "múinín" (muinín); "ina gcuid dtíortha féin" (ina dtíortha féin); "mar gheall mar go bhfuil"; "píonós"; "An aontaíonn tú leis an tuairimí" (leis na tuairimí); "Cá s é / í" is garbled.

- **Gramadach na Gaeilge 2:** "shuí sé síos" (shuigh).
- **Achoimre:** "an t-airgead go dógfaidh sé" (dtógfaidh); several lines are cut off mid-word.
- **AS A2 Léamh:** "tuistí" in the text but "tuismitheoirí" in the answer list; UCAS in the text but CAO in the cloze; "ar ceisteanna" (ar cheisteanna); "Cuir isteach isteach"; "I do chuid" (i do chuid).

The app's Year 8 builder has "i nDroimeann Tí" for Dromintee but McKeown gives "Droim an Tí", so one should be settled against logainm.ie.

- **I mo:** "a (her) provection" is the wrong term (the rule is no change before a consonant, h before a vowel); "Is she is sitting".
- **MA translations:** typing slips throughout, for example "bith bith", "taithi", "foreigean", "íonsaí", "duradh léí", "í gceist", "áitíuil", "ag teach" (ag teacht), "éannuiar", "fúithí", "Cá mhéad".

### 2.4 Overlaps and duplicates

- **Exact duplicates:** Achoimre and Achoimre 3 (one blank line apart); Dlí 1 and Dlí1a are two drafts of the same legal translation; gramadach IMU/RELATIVE duplicates the top-level RELATIVE.doc.
- **The 32-county list** appears word for word in Nótaí Comhrá and Dioplóma 1, and partly in Bailte Móra.
- **Days and months** appear in Nótaí Comhrá (a clear table with the gender and genitive of each month), Féilire (images), Gramadach 1 (etymologies) and UIMHREACHA & Laethanta.
- **Counting and personal numbers** appear in Counting in Irish, ag comhaireamh daoine, Nótaí Gramadaí "Na hUimhreacha Pearsanta" and Achoimre.
- **Grammar coverage:** Nótaí Gramadaí is the grammar strand of the same diploma whose syllabus is in Dioplóma 1 ("Míreanna Teanga"). It overlaps Gramadach 1 (the declensions) and Achoimre (the mutation rules). The noun and verb agents' files will overlap all three.
- **Le.doc and Le - míniú.doc** are two versions of the same Year 8 sheet.

### 2.5 Suitability and sensitivity

The diploma conversation notes were written for adults in 2003 and are anchored in the Republic: RTÉ, the Late Late Show, TV3, Bertie Ahern and Celia, George W. Bush, the Gardaí, neutrality and NATO, Sellafield, CD buying. Some statements are unsuitable for pupils as written: "Bheinn sásta duine ó chine eile (duine dubh, mar shampla) a phósadh", "Bheinn féin sásta siúl amach le taistealaí", and "féile mhór na drúise" (Valentine's Day). They could be reworked for a Northern Ireland school in the 2020s, but not copied. The Women's Aid leaflet in the MA folder is sensitive and adult; leave it out.

## 3. Proposed arrangement

1. **Teacher reference shelf (not pupil-facing).** Deismir (the Ulster idiom source), Nótaí Gramadaí (diploma) and Achoimre (a checklist only; mark it "Munster/Connacht: do not copy forms"), and An Séimhiú (background).

## 4. Opportunities for the app (concrete)

### 4.1 Comhrá AS: opinion and reaction phrase bank (all 16 topics)

From Nótaí Comhrá (a box of reactions for pair discussion):

> tá sé sin fíor go pointe / is fíor duit / tagaim leis an mhéid sin / a mhalairt atá fíor / tá mé idir dhá chomhairle faoi sin / níl mé ar aon intinn leat faoi sin / sin díreach é

and the five-point agreement key: "Aontaím amach is amach ... Tá mé idir dhá chomhairle ... Easaontaím amach is amach." Add a shared "Tuairim" row, or a set of optional opener tiles, to every Comhrá builder. The app has "go pointe" twice and none of the others.

### 4.2 Comhrá AS: Ulster idioms that lift an answer (curated from Deismir)

Pick three to five per topic, rewritten in modern Ulster spelling, flagged as optional "extra marks" tiles, and credited to *Deismir* (Mac Giolla Chomhaill, 2002). Candidates, all found in the file:

- **Sláinte mheabhrach / sláinte:** "is fearr an tsláinte ná na táinte"; "tá sláinte an bhradáin aici"; "faigheann foighid fortacht".
- **Costas maireachtála:** "ní ar bharr copóg a gheibhtear an t-airgead" (money doesn't grow on trees); "tá a thrí luach ar achan sórt ar na saolta seo"; "ní furasta fuineadh de réir na mine ar na saolta seo" (living within one's means).
- **An scoil / pleananna:** "rinne sí a seacht ndícheall"; "beidh mé ar mo bhionda feasta (dhéanfaidh mé mo dhícheall as seo amach)"; "tá ciall cheannaithe agam ón lá sin amach"; "d'éirigh thar barr leis".
- **An Ghaeilge:** "chuaigh muid go Rann na Feirste d'fhonn snas a chur ar ár gcuid Gaeilge"; "tá lúth na teanga aici".
- **Saoire agus taisteal:** "bhí mé ar mo sháimhín só ar an trá"; "castar na daoine ar a chéile ach ní chastar na cnoic ná na sléibhte"; "tá sé ag cur sceana gréasaí" (raining cats and dogs).
- **Mo cheantar:** "ní bhfaighidh tú áit níos fearr ar dhroim an domhain"; "comharsa béal dorais".
- **Teaghlach:** "mar a bíos an cú, bíonn an coileán" (like father, like son); "tá siad amuigh ar a chéile" (not on speaking terms).
- **Spórt:** "ní bhfuair sé cothrom na Féinne"; "thug sí tomhas a láimhe féin dó".

### 4.3 Comhrá AS: follow-up questions and new topic ideas (from Nótaí Comhrá)

Rewrite these in Ulster (Céard becomes Cad é; bpá becomes phá) and add them to `questions`:

- **as-saoire:** "Cén turas is fearr nó is measa a rinne tú riamh? Cad chuige?"; "Cad iad na rudaí is mó a chuireann isteach ort agus tú ar laethanta saoire?"; "Dá mbeadh do rogha saoire agat, cá rachfá?"
- **as-post:** "Cad iad na huaireanta agus na laethanta a d'oibrigh tú?"; "An raibh tú sásta leis an phá?"; "An molfá an post do dhaoine eile?". Also the work vocabulary quiz (*saoiste, ragobair, tuarastal, ardú céime, comhoibrithe, folúntas*) and the "rank what matters in a job" task.
- **as-caitheamh (ceol):** "Cén cineál ceoil nach maith leat ar chor ar bith? Cad chuige?"; "An seinneann tú aon uirlis? Cén uirlis ar mhaith leat a bheith ábalta a sheinm?"
- **as-ceantar:** "Dá dtiocfadh leat cónaí in áit ar bith, cén áit a roghnófá?"; "Cad iad na buntáistí agus na míbhuntáistí a bhaineann le bheith i do chónaí faoin tuath?"; the statement "Is ceantar maith é le páistí a thógáil ann."
- **as-meain:** "Cén clár teilifíse a chuaigh i bhfeidhm ort?" (a Dioplóma 2 essay title); the advertising questions ("Ar cheannaigh tú rud riamh mar gur spreag fógra thú?").
- **as-gaeilge:** "'Tír gan teanga, tír gan anam.' An aontaíonn tú?"; "Braitheann todhchaí na Gaeilge ar na Gaeltachtaí."
- **as-slainte-mheabhrach:** statements from "Cén Cineál Duine Tú?": "Ní bhraithim strus i mo shaol", "Is breá liom am a chaitheamh i m'aonar", "Cuireann rudaí a deir daoine eile isteach orm".
- **as-aos-og:** problem-page scenarios from Dioplóma 2 "Réiteach Fadhbanna / Céard a dhéanfaidh mé?", for example noisy neighbours, rewritten for teenagers. They practise giving advice with the conditional: "Dá mbeinn i do bhróga, ...".
- **A possible seventeenth topic:** "Ceiliúradh agus féilte" (Valentine's Day, Christmas, Easter, from the reported-speech drill), or "An timpeallacht" as a stand-alone topic, using the environment statements refreshed for today.

### 4.4 Comhrá AS: film and TV review phrases (Dioplóma 1, "Abairtí Úsáideacha don Léirmheas")

### 4.5 A new "Aontaigh nó easaontaigh" practice mode (AS)

The diploma's rating-card format is a strong, reusable task: the pupil sees a statement, chooses a position on the five-point key, then builds a reasoned answer from the phrase bank plus one idiom. The source statements should be replaced with ones for young people in Northern Ireland today (TG4, AI, phones in school, sport, part-time work, cost of living), which fit the existing topics.

### 4.6 Year 8: days of the week and dates

Confirmed gap: `units.json` has the months (for birthdays) but no days apart from "Satharn". From Nótaí Comhrá:

From Féilire: "An t-aonú lá is fiche de Bhealtaine". Add a days row to "Mo lá" ("Dé Luain téim ...") and an optional exact-date chunk to the birthday sentence in "Mé féin" ("Bíonn mo lá breithe ar an cheathrú lá de mhí Iúil"). ### 4.7 Year 8: where I live (counties, towns and Armagh place names)

The "Mo cheantar" builder already has the local villages (sa Sruthán, i gCamloch, sa Mhaigh ...). Bailte Móra adds the next layer: counties and towns with the correct "go" and "i" forms:

> go hArd Mhacha / in Ard Mhacha; go Contae an Dúin / i gContae an Dúin; go dtí an Lorgain / ar an Lorgain; i bPort an Dúnáin

That gives the sentences "Tá mé i mo chónaí sa Sruthán, i gContae Ard Mhacha" and "Téim go Béal Feirste / go dtí an Iúr". Logainmneacha adds meanings pupils remember ("An Céide: flat-topped hill"; "Crois Mhic Lionnáin: Lennon's cross"; "Béal Átha an Airgid: ford-mouth of the silver"). Check every form against logainm.ie first (see 2.3). The blank Éire map could become a tap-the-county labelling activity.

### 4.8 A translation practice type (grammar track)

The notes are full of English-to-Irish sentence drills that match the app's lessons one to one:

- Aistrigh (Ar / Níor / Nár) fits r1-chaite "Ceist le Ar, diúltach le Níor". Example: "Did he buy the book?" becomes "Ar cheannaigh sé an leabhar?"
- The Gramadach 1 "Cuir Gaeilge ar" sets fit the present, past and future of both conjugations ("We will put the books into the bag").
- The I mo grid fits "sealbhach: I mo chónaí, ina shuí" ("She lives in Armagh but she's from Belfast").
- The Nótaí Gramadaí translation sections fit the autonomous verb and the genitive.

Build them as tile-assembly or typed answers. Every key must follow the rulings (*druid, cluin, tábla, muid*).

### 4.9 An AS/A2 reading comprehension template (AS A2 Léamh)

McKeown's five-part structure makes a ready-made format: (a) make a sentence with each key word, (b) swap in a synonym from a list, (c) finish a sentence in your own words, (d) translate a paragraph into English, and (e) a cloze with grammatical hints such as "(an tír, f2, ginideach uatha)" or "(bí, an aimsir fháistineach)". The hint in part (e) matches the app's lesson labels, so a wrong answer can link straight to the lesson. New texts are needed; McKeown's Ulster translations are the obvious candidates if he agrees (the Rathlin lighthouse and the Glen Malin walk suit as-ceantar and as-saoire; "Juggernaut in Burg" suits moving abroad).

### 4.10 An error-correction practice type (A2 accuracy)

Dioplóma 2 prints a passage with its errors numbered: "Bainiú (1) geit as mhuintir (2) an Iarthar (3) le deireanaí nuair a tháinig sé chun solas (4) ...". Tapping a numbered word and choosing the correction is an ideal mobile task. The teacher should write Ulster passages seeded with the errors pupils actually make (séimhiú after sa and don, the genitive after the verbal noun, "Ar/Níor").

### 4.11 Smaller wins

- **Question words** (Míreanna ceisteacha): a "What is the examiner asking?" listening drill for Comhrá (*Cad chuige, Cén dóigh, Cad é mar*), which also suits Year 8.
- **Alliterative adjectives** (IMU "Féith na filíochta"): "fuar fliuch feanntach, beag bídeach, sona sásta, dorcha duairc" as optional tiles in Year 8 "Cur síos orm féin" and in any weather row. The IMU exercise "Ghluais an sionnach glic gasta síos an bóthar cúng cásta go ciúin cúramach" shows the pay-off.
- **Reported speech pair drill** (Nótaí Gramadaí: ask, then "Cad é a dúirt sé?") as a Comhrá warm-up linked to "Sílim gur ...".
- **Health quiz** ("Cé chomh sláintiúil is atá tusa?") as a warm-up for as-slainte-mheabhrach.
