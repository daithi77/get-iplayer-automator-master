# Reference corpora in the old Grammar zip: assessment for Gan Ainm

**Scope:** Grammar/taisce/, Grammar/Fiontar/, An tOllchnuasach 1985-2000, An tOllchnuasach Leasaithe 2005, Cruinnscriobh.pdf, Glancecard.pdf, foclóir sláinte.doc, foclóir bitheolaíochta.doc, FoclóirReiligiúinMaterDei.rtf.

**Method:**
- I read the title, preface and information pages.
- I counted entries from table rows and lines.
- I compared samples with the Comhrá AS unit titles in builders/as-comhra.json.
- I ran text recognition on the Glancecard, which is image only.
- This was read-only.

## Bottom line

All of this is published by other people: none of it is Dáithí's own work. Most of it carries an explicit "all rights reserved" notice from Foras na Gaeilge, Cois Life or Leon Mac Aogáin. All of it dates from 1990 to 2005. The national terminology database, tearma.ie, has since taken in the Coiste Téarmaíochta and FIONTAR content and replaced it.

**Do not import, bundle or bulk-extract any of it into the app.** Its legitimate uses are narrow:
- Dáithí can look through it by hand to decide which concepts a Comhrá vocabulary list should cover.
- It can give a second opinion when checking the app's Irish.

Every term that goes into the app should be checked on tearma.ie, and on focloir.ie or teanglann.ie for everyday usage and Ulster audio.

## 2. Currency: are these superseded?

**Yes, as a source to rely on.** As I understand the history:
- In 2006 Foras na Gaeilge and FIONTAR (DCU) launched focal.ie, the national terminology database.
- focal.ie took in the Coiste dictionaries, the Ollchnuasach and the FIONTAR term bank, which is exactly this material.
- focal.ie became **tearma.ie**, which the Coiste keeps up to date with status marks and new decisions.
- For everyday (non-technical) English-Irish, the modern standard is **focloir.ie** (Foras na Gaeilge, 2013).
- **teanglann.ie** has the � - Dónaill and de Bhaldraithe dictionaries and pronunciation, including Ulster audio.

I could not open tearma.ie from this sandbox because the network proxy blocks it. The comparisons below come from the files alone; look up each flagged term on tearma.ie before relying on it.

**Signs of age in the files:**
- **Terms still in flux:**
  - "download" is given as "lódáil v anuas, íoslódáil v", and "automatic download" as "uathlódáil f3 anuas" (riomh).
  - "audio download" is "íoschóip f2 fuaime / íoschóipeáil v" (Ollchnuasach).
  - "upload" is "lódáil v suas, uaslódáil v".
  - None of these sources settles on a single form, such as íoslódáil and uaslódáil.
- **"Web site"** is given as "láithreán m1 Gréasáin, suíomh m1 Gréasáin", with a capital G and two options. Current usage is the lower-case "suíomh gréasáin". The riomh preface admits it gave pairs such as "luch/luchóg, láithreán/suíomh, cliceáil/gliogáil".
- **"mental health"** has three competing forms: "sláinte f4 intinne; sláinte f4 mheabhrach" (fiseol) and "meabhairshláinte f4 [leagan RaA]" (Ollchnuasach 2005). The app's "sláinte mheabhrach" has support here, but check tearma.ie for the currently preferred form.
- **Obsolete technology:** "cassette recorder: taifeadán m1 caiséid" and "teletext: teilitéacs m4" (teilifis); "daisy-wheel printer: printéir roth nóinín" and "floppy diskettes" (riomh); "Cyber Space Café: Caifé Cibearspáis" and "Internet café: caifé Idirlín" (Ollchnuasach).
- **Missing concepts pupils need now:**
  - No hits in any corpus for smartphone, social media, social network, selfie, influencer, cyberbullying, podcast, hashtag, emoji, chatbot, streaming service, climate crisis, carbon footprint or food bank.
  - "app" appears only as "application software".
  - "cost of living" appears only in the finance term "cost of living adjustment: coigeartú costais mhaireachtála" (FIONTAR).
  - Four of the sixteen Comhrá units are therefore the worst covered: An teicneolaíocht, An intleacht shaorga, TG4 scannáin agus sruthú, and Costas maireachtála.
- **Dated or offensive labels:** "mental defective: meabhairéalangach m1" and "mental handicap: bac m1 meabhrach" (fiseol). These must not reach pupils.
- **Dialect:**
  - All of it is standard (Caighdeán Oifigiúil) terminology, not Ulster usage.
  - The app uses the Ulster "guthán" four times, where these sources would push towards "fón" ("fóin siúil", "fóin phóca").
  - Comhrá material should keep the natural Ulster forms and use a standard term only where there is no everyday alternative.

**How each possible use stands up:**
- **Comhrá AS vocabulary list:** worth doing, but as the app's own list, written in Dáithí's Ulster voice and checked term by term on tearma.ie, focloir.ie and teanglann.ie. The old corpora can suggest concepts (piarbhrú, neamhord itheacháin, athrú aeráide). They cannot supply the newest vocabulary, which is what pupils will actually talk about.
- **Science or subject vocabulary:** low value. Pupils in an English-medium school learn science in English. The terms are very technical (for example "acanthocephalous: acantaiceifileach"), and copying them would mean bulk extraction from a copyrighted database. If it were ever needed, link out to tearma.ie.
- **Checking the app's Irish:** the best use, done by hand. The corpora confirm gender and declension notes (m1, f3, gs), which also helps the grammar track. For anything that ships, tearma.ie is the authority because it records later decisions.
- **Cruinnscríobh na Gaeilge:** a strong reference for checking the grammar track. Section 5.4, "Réamhfhocail shimplí agus an t-alt uatha: canúint Chúige Uladh", is directly relevant to Ulster pupils. Consult it, but don't copy its text or examples.
- **Glancecard:** a commercial verb chart. The app can present verbs in its own format, because the grammar itself is common knowledge. It must not reproduce the card's layout, colour scheme or text.
- **Foclóir Reiligiúin:** only relevant to Religious Education through Irish; not a Comhrá resource.
- **TV studio phrases (teilifis/leagan.htm):** written for production crews and not relevant to pupils.

## 4. Recommendation

1. **Put none of this material in the repo or the app:** no word lists, no JSON built from it, no PDFs.
   - Everything here belongs to someone else and is marked "Gach ceart ar cosaint".
   - The unmarked lists come from the same Foras na Gaeilge and DCU databases.
   - Systematically extracting from them would raise database-right issues as well as copyright.
   - Owning a copy gives no right to republish it.
2. **Write the Comhrá vocabulary lists as original content.** Choose the concepts for each unit, write natural Ulster Irish, and check every technical term on tearma.ie (with focloir.ie or teanglann.ie for register and pronunciation). Using individual checked words is fine; copying the lists is not.
3. **Keep the corpora offline as Dáithí's private desk reference,** especially Cruinnscríobh for the grammar track. Treat every computing, media or health term from 1985 to 2005 as "check before use".
4. **Remove the duplicates if tidying the zip:** Grammar/Fiontar/ copies taisce/obair/, and the two Word glossaries copy taisce/fiseol and taisce/bitheol.
5. **Avoid in pupil-facing material:**
   - the dated health labels ("meabhairéalangach", "bac meabhrach");
   - obsolete technology terms;
   - competing variants such as "íoschóipeáil" and "lódáil anuas";
   - replacing the Ulster "guthán" with the standard "fón".
6. **For new concepts with no settled term** (cyberbullying, influencer, cost-of-living crisis), look them up on tearma.ie. If a term isn't there, send a query to the Coiste Téarmaíochta through tearma.ie rather than coining one from these old sources.
