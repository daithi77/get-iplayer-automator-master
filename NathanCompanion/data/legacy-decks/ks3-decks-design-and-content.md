# KS3 group: design, pedagogy and content report (44 files)

## Summary (under 250 words)

The 44 files hold about 31 distinct decks. **18 are Dáithí's own**: 13 Keynotes from 2013 and 5 from 2009, in 25 files. **11 are by colleagues**: Mr McNamee, Gerard McKeown, Gabhán Ó Dochartaigh, "cblaney024" and "ST3-5230218", in 17 files. The other 2 files are the 2009 school PowerPoint template (Conor Starrs).

His own decks have a clear grammar:
- **One idea per slide.** A picture sits above a single short Irish caption. 14 of his 18 decks do this.
- **Title slide with a year tag.** Irish title, small English gloss, a "Bliain 8" tab and the crest. 11 of 18.
- **Presentation, then retrieval.** Items appear in order, again in order, then shuffled or picture-only. 4 of 18.
- **Colour marks the part that changes.** Red on the slot word, colour-coded chunks, the eclipsing letter picked out.
- **Local transfer.** Local townlands with eclipsis, a photo of the school, class codes, a phone number and a car registration.
- **"Dul siar" review list** (4 of 18) and a **bookend closing slide** that repeats the title (6 of 18).
- **One full lesson arc (Mé Féin 2).** Ten numbered questions build to model paragraphs by local pupils, then "Feabhas? Can we improve this?", then "Inis do scéal féin".

"Sprioc foghlama" appears in only 2 of 18, both from 2013. It is an intention he was moving towards, not yet a habit.

The colleagues' decks are dense bullet lists on clipart backgrounds. Several are KS4 level. They contain real errors (Rithfim, Íthim, Carb as, Dearthair, bord).

Rights problems are serious. The decks use about 30 Simpsons images, Bugs Bunny, celebrity photos (Obama, Ed Sheeran, Beckham), hotlinked Google images and a ripped commercial song recording. None of these can go into the app.

The best new units, by value against effort: **Fáilte** (greetings and feelings), **Sa seomra ranga** (classroom commands), **Uimhreacha** (with real-world numbers), then **Mo theach**.

### 1.4 Typical sequence
The canonical arc comes from Mé Féin 1 and 2, with Uimhreacha as the drill model:

1. Crest slide, then title with year tag and part number.
2. (Sprioc foghlama, from 2013 onwards.)
3. Question 1 as a section header, then the answer, modelled by a character in a speech bubble.
4. One item per slide, building up the answer set.
5. Decomposition: colour-coded chunks of the target sentence.
6. Local transfer: pupils' own towns, school and class.
7. Rule and mnemonic where a mutation appears.
8. Retrieval: in order, again, then shuffled.
9. Dul siar: all the questions so far, numbered.
10. Model paragraphs, then "Can we improve this?", then "Tell your own story".
11. Bookend title.

### 1.5 Colour
- **Red for the part that changes.** In Mé Féin 2 the pet noun is in red ("Tá **madadh** agam"), and so is the personal number ("Tá **cúigear** i mo theaghlach"). The eclipsing letters in the i + place-name table are in red. "i Springfield" is in blue as the slot.
- **Chunk colours.** The age sentence uses purple / red / green / red / blue, one per chunk.
- **Question vs answer.** In Cad é mar atá tú the question is always dark red and the answers black; "agus tusa...?" is blue.
- **Dul siar bands.** Each question sits in its own saturated band (purple, red, cyan, green, magenta).
- **McKeown's habitual Ar Maidin** does the same thing more systematically: verb ending in red and underlined, mutation or initial letter in green ("An **d**téann tú ... Ní t**h**éim").
- Dated uses to drop: rainbow letters in titles (Dialann an Lae), WordArt-style titles, saturated full-slide backgrounds (yellow, lime, navy gradient) in the colleagues' decks.

### 1.6 Typography and density
- His 2013 decks use one sans-serif, centred, roughly 28 to 40 pt, one line under the picture. Nearly all vocabulary slides carry 1 to 5 words.
- Only Dul siar and the model paragraphs carry more than a line.
- The colleagues' decks run 8 to 12 bullets of 14 to 18 pt on textured backgrounds.

### 1.7 Irish and English
- Irish first and dominant.
- English appears only as a small grey subtitle ("Myself", "Revision", "Downstairs", "Now tell your own story"), in the Sprioc (all English), and in rule explanations.
- No slide in his decks translates each Irish item. The picture carries the meaning.

### 1.8 Interactivity and distinctive traits
- Heavy use of builds for reveal.
- Picture-before-word prediction.
- Shuffled recall and picture-only drills.
- Speech-bubble dialogues to model Q and A.
- A song with audio.
- A hyperlink to logainm.ie, which sends pupils to an authoritative source.
- Humour and pop culture: Simpsons characters as the "speakers"; Ed Sheeran for "rua" and Beckham for "fionn".
- Relentlessly local: Bessbrook, Camlough, Newry, the school building, real class codes.
- Two thirds of his decks are single-topic vocabulary decks. The fuller pedagogy (questions, then decomposition, then model, then improve) appears only in Mé Féin. That is his "ambition and intent".

## 4. Recommendations for Gan Ainm in 2026

### 4a. House-style spec (teacher slides "Don rang" and pupil screens)

**Principles to keep from him:** one idea per screen; picture carries meaning, not English; the thing that changes is visibly marked; see, then predict, then reveal, then retrieve in random order; bring it home (local places, the school, real numbers); end with review, a model and "improve it"; bookend.

**Principles to drop:** themed backgrounds, placeholder photos, cartoon characters, celebrities, rainbow lettering, a crest on every slide, English-only Sprioc.

**Slide types** (build these as `t:` kinds in deckHTML, the way gdeckHTML already works):

1. **Teideal.**
   - Irish title (72 to 96 px on a 1080p screen); English gloss at 40% size in muted grey.
   - Chips "Bliain 8" and "Aonad 3" (and "Cuid 1" if split). The chips replace his rotated tabs.
   - One illustration or the unit emoji.
   - The crest only here and on Críoch.
2. **Sprioc foghlama.**
   - 2 or 3 "Beidh mé ábalta ..." lines in Irish, with English behind the existing "Taispeáin an Béarla" toggle.
   - Optional "Obair bhaile" line, as in Orduithe.
3. **Ceisteanna.** The unit's numbered question spine (exists; keep the numbers, as in Mé Féin 1 to 10).
4. **Réamhamharc (predict).** Picture or English cue only. The first clicker press reveals the Irish and plays the audio; the next press moves on. This is the same mechanic gdeck already uses.
5. **Tógáil (builder row)** (exists).
   - Fixed chunks in ink.
   - The slot column's chosen chunk in `--pen` red and bold, with an underline so it is not colour-only.
   - Column tints `k0` to `k3` keep one role per colour across all units (for example k0 frame, k1 slot, k2 qualifier, k3 time or reason).
6. **Briseadh síos (decomposition).** A whole model sentence, then the same sentence split into tinted chunks, one per build (Mé Féin age slides).
7. **Riail + cuimhneachán (rule + mnemonic).** A short rule, a table (e.g. i + logainm with the eclipsing letter in `--pen`), one mnemonic, voiced.
8. **Druil (drill).** Shuffled rapid recall.
   - Picture-only or English-only cue, optional 3 to 5 s auto-advance, then an in-order pass.
   - This digitises the "gan focail" and Uimhreacha decks.
9. **Saol na fírinne (real world).** Local transfer: townlands, the school, class codes, phone numbers, prices.
10. **Dul siar.** Numbered questions in one tint (not rainbow bands); tap to hear; click to reveal a model answer.
11. **Samhail agus feabhas (model + improve).**
    - A model paragraph by a named local pupil, built from the unit's rows.
    - Then "Feabhas? Can we improve this?" with 2 or 3 prompts (add "agus", "mar", a time phrase).
12. **Inis do scéal féin.** Prompt slide, which hands off to a pupil "Mo scéal" screen.
13. **Amhrán (song, optional).** Line-by-line lyric with audio, full verse, then a gap-fill.
14. **Críoch.** A bookend repeating the title, with "Na sleamhnáin eile".

**Canonical sequence:** 1, 2, 3, then for each row 4 and 5 (6 for the first row), then 7 where a mutation arises, then 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 14. The current deck (title, rows, model answers) lacks 2, 4, 6 to 11 and 14.

**Colour roles** (using the existing tokens, light and dark):

| Role | Token |
