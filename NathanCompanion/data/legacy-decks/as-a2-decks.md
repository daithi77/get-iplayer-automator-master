# AS and A2 legacy decks: pedagogy, layout and what Gan Ainm should take from them

## Summary

The group has 24 files and 346 slides. Once exact copies, a misfiled GCSE deck and a Simpsons picture deck are set aside, 160 distinct AS and A2 slides remain. Twelve files were written by Gerard Mc Keown in 2004. Seven are Dáithí's own or his adaptations. Five came from other people: a 2007 university training deck by Barra Ó hEireamhóin, a novel study from Coláiste Chaitríona, two magazine articles, and exam-board mark schemes.

His method is consistent across the decks:
- **Five questions for any picture stimulus.** Describe, react, read, infer, then bring in your own experience. His Keynote calls this "The Five Finger Approach".
- **Model answers built beside the picture.** The stimulus stays on screen as a thumbnail while the answer builds next to it, a few lines per slide.
- **Alternatives separated by slashes.** These are sentence builders in all but name, which fits the Conti method the app already follows.
- **Opinion and both sides.** He requires an opinion and models both sides of the argument.
- **Reassurance in Irish.** Lines such as "Tóg do chuid ama", "Tá cead agat...", and a proverb to close each deck.
- **A fixed six-step reading sequence**, ending with grammar in context.

The stimulus sheets inside the decks follow the same layout as his scanned worksheets: bold serif title, large photo with caption, then two to four short paragraphs ending on a question.

About one slide in five breaks a standing ruling or is out of date:
- **Exam board and marks.** The board's name, web links, weightings, deadlines and verbatim band descriptors.
- **Dead sources and old facts.** The Celtic Tiger, the euro, "TG4 at ten", and websites that no longer exist.
- **Language errors.** "Dídeanaithe" (refugees) is used for homeless people, and several non-Ulster forms appear.
- **Third-party material.** Press photos, articles and clipart the app cannot reuse.

Ranked for the app:
1. Follow-up questions on existing topics.
2. A cross-topic opinion chunk bank.
3. A five-finger picture-stimulus mode.
4. Four new Comhrá topics: homelessness, the tobacco and vaping ban, drugs discussed in the third person, and Loch nEathach.
5. A reading-passage mode.
6. A presentation builder.
7. An A2 track.

### 1.3 Sequences he uses

- **Exam deck.** Title; the parts of the exam; for each part, what it is, then a reassurance slide ("Tóg do chuid ama!", "Ná bí buartha!", "Tá cead agat bheith ginearálta..."), then the mark scheme, then the method, then a sample; the next part; a closing proverb and good-luck slide ("Cleachtadh a dhéanann máistreacht", "Ní mar a shíltear a bhítear" beside a goblin cartoon of the visiting examiner, "Ádh mór oraibh go léir").
- **Stimulus model deck.** The full stimulus; the title and first impression ("Is léir dom go bhfuil baint ag an phictiúr seo le..."); "Cuir síos air" over two slides; the text and its message; opinion and experience; a solution. This is the five-finger method carried out.
- **Five-finger method** (A2 slide 8, "Cur chuige amháin", which became "The Five Finger Approach"):
  1. Cad é a fheiceann tú?
  2. Cad é do bharúil faoin rud a fheiceann tú?
  3. Cad é atá scríofa?
  4. Cén teachtaireacht atá taobh thiar den rud atá scríofa?
  5. Cad é do thaithí nó do bharúil féin?

  The Fís deck gives a phrase bank for each step: Tús; An pictiúr féin (with position words: sa lár, sa chúlra, chun tosaigh); An téacs; An teachtaireacht; Do thaithí.
- **Reading deck.** The article, with target chunks underlined; then six tasks: (a) a sentence of your own with each chunk; (b) word-bank synonym gap-fill; (c) say it another way; (d) finish the sentence in your own words; (e) exact English translation; (f) grammar cloze with cues in brackets ("beár: m1, ginideach uatha", "scríobh: saorbhriathar san aimsir chaite"). The tobacco deck starts with comprehension questions "i d'fhocail féin".
- **Role-play.** A task card with four prompts, then one slide per prompt with three or four answers in different colours at rising difficulty.
- **Presentation.** Four or five points in order; facts with a named source; an opinion on each point; a model introduction ("Dia duit. Is mise... ba mhaith liom labhairt leat inniu faoi... Ba mhaith liom díriú go háirithe ar..."); a model evidence paragraph ("De réir staitisticí a chuir... amach anuraidh... Sílim gur staitistic scannalach í seo. Níl aon dabht i m'intinn ná go luíonn cuid mhaith den locht ar..."); questions for the short discussion afterwards, about why and how the pupil researched the topic.

### 1.4 How he scaffolds opinion and discussion

- **Always an opinion.** "Have an opinion or at least pretend to have an opinion!", "Moltar duit do bharúil féin a chur in iúl", "BÍ GLÓRACH".
- **Both sides modelled on one slide.** The Irish-online model gives "Aontaím leis sin mar..." and "Ní aontaím leis sin mar..." side by side. The boxing model runs "Measann cuid mhór daoine... Síleann daoine áirithe... ach tá mise den bharúil go bhfuil dul amú orthu". The tobacco article frames "Tá siad siúd atá i bhfábhar an dlí ag rá..." against the publicans' view.
- **Hedged inference.** "Déarfainn féin gur glacadh an pictiúr i...", "b'fhéidir", "Is dóigh liom gur siombal é seo do...", "Chítear codarsnacht sa phictiúr seo: ar láimh amháin... ar an láimh eile...".
- **Personal experience as evidence.** "I mo thaithí féin", "Is cuimhin liom uair amháin", "Chonaic mé clár faisnéise...", "Baintear stad asam nuair a...".
- **A solution to end on.** "Sílim gur cheart...", "Ceist is doiligh a fhreagairt".
- **A conversation, not a question-and-answer session.** Develop your answers, correct yourself, and do not lean on prepared monologues.
- **Graded alternatives** in the role-plays and slash options in the phrase banks. These are sentence-builder columns in all but name.

### 1.5 The worksheet pattern, confirmed inside the decks

The four stimulus sheets embedded as slide images (homelessness, drugs, boxing, Irish online) share the layout of "Beo bocht", "Gaelsaoire" and "Doirteadh Ola":
- a bold serif or display title that states a tension or asks a question: "Spórt nó brúidiúlacht?", "Do Rogha, Do Bheatha", "Tá sé in am gníomhú", "An Dúlra i mBaol";
- one large photo or a collage of up to four;
- two to four short paragraphs of AS-level Irish, 80 to 150 words;
- a closing rhetorical question ("cad é mar is féidir mí-úsáid na ndrugaí seo a chosc?", "Nach bocht an comhartha é...?", "Ní lia duine ná barúil.").

The worksheet titles use a colon ("Gaelsaoire: Scíth sa Ghaeltacht"), which is the natural replacement for the dashes the ruling bans.

### 3.2 Non-Ulster forms

- The Australian football article is in Connacht Irish: "baint éicint", "ar an gcluiche", "sa gcluiche", "an gcaid", "i leaba", "léimt". The vocabulary exercise repeats "éicint".
- *Gafa* quotes the novel's Connacht forms ("céard", "ag an bpointe", "don bhfearg", "conas"). This is acceptable inside quotation marks but not in pupil models.
- "freisin" appears 7 times, all in the two magazine articles. "fosta" appears 13 times in the models, which is correct.
- "Ceapaim" and "Ní cheapaim" appear where Ulster prefers "Sílim" and "Ní shílim".
- The Gafa deck has "Faighimid". Under the verb-form ruling the default is "faigheann muid".
- Positives, to keep: cad é mar, cad chuige, bomaite, doiligh, thig liom, thiocfadh leat, achan, s'agam, fá, "bheas", "tábla" (in *Gafa* and the handout). There is no "dún", "clois", "bord" or "-amar" anywhere.

### 3.3 Ruling breaches

- **The exam board.** Its name and web address appear on 8 slides in the A2 and AS decks. "Submit your topic and title to [board]" is a deadline instruction. Unit and paper names, weightings (17.5%, 5%, 7.5%, 26%, 46%, marks out of 30 and 40), specification wording quoted in English ("A bank of 10 items... will not exceed 120"), the specification's theme headings, and band descriptors quoted or screenshotted (6 slides) all appear. **None of this can go into the app.** Success criteria should be restated in Dáithí's own Irish.
- **Dashes.** En dashes appear in about 30 titles and bullets ("Ná bí faiteach  -  BÍ GLÓRACH", "Dífhostaíocht  -  beo go deo", "Fail to prepare  -  prepare to fail"). Replace them with a colon or a question.
- **"student".** The word appears in the English specification lists. Use "pupil".

### 3.4 Dated content (2002 to 2013)

- **Exam format.** Timings, the pro-forma word count, submission deadlines, the visiting-examiner briefing, 20-minute slots, and role-play tasks (no longer set) all belong to a superseded specification.
- **Sources that no longer exist in the form named.** Lá (nuacht.com), Foinse, Beo! (beo.ie), Cumasc, Mílaoise na Gaeltachta and Litríocht.com. Raidió na Gaeltachta, TG4, An tUltach and BBC Radio Ulster's Irish programmes are current.
- **Economics.** The Celtic Tiger, "Tionchar an Euro ar Gheilleagar na hÉireann", "Is beag teach anois nach bhfuil ríomhaire ann".
- **Media and phones.** "TG4: Deich mBliana Linn". TG4 turns 30 in October 2026, which is a better hook. "Fóin phóca" is now about smartphones in school.
- **Tobacco.** The trade union's 2003 claim of 150 deaths a year among its members; the Republic's 2003 to 2004 bill; publicans proposing smoking rooms. The North's ban on smoking in enclosed public places dates from 30 April 2007. The current debates are the proposed UK generational ban and vaping (section 4a).
- **The statistic "5,840 teenage heroin addicts... 75% worse than anywhere in the world"**, credited to a named foundation. It looks invented and should never be shown as fact. It could be kept only as a warning example of a "statistic" nobody can check.
- **Named people and events.** The Australian football history is fine but old. The boxing sheet shows real, identifiable professional boxers.
- **Safeguarding for independent pupil use.** Several titles need a teacher present: "An Féinmharú in Éirinn", "Féinghortú agus féinmharú", "Cailíní sna déaga ag iompar clainne", "Boyfriend, girlfriend" topics, and "Ollscoil nó Ólscoil?" (given the ruling against drinking in model answers).

### 3.5 Third-party material and rights

- **Magazine articles** reproduced in full (2002 and 2003): do not reuse. Write new passages in the same pattern.
- **Stimulus sheets.** These look like published or specimen stimulus items with press photographs: identifiable boxers and sponsor logos, a homeless man, drug paraphernalia, 2004 website screenshots. Do not reuse them.
- **Mark scheme tables and specification text.** These are exam-board copyright, and naming the board also breaks the ruling.
- **Barra Ó hEireamhóin's 2007 deck** underlies both A2 decks, and the credit was removed in the 2008 copy. If anything close to it is republished, credit him or rewrite it.
- **Other colleagues' and schools' work:** Gerard Mc Keown's 2004 decks, apparently made for a Southern Education and Library Board support day; the author "TH" of the AS base deck; the *Gafa* deck from Coláiste Chaitríona. Treat these as colleague-shared teaching material. Ask before reusing their text in a distributed app.
- **Images.** Office clipart (syringe, cartoon faces, eyes, scrolls, the goblin examiner), stock photos (the wheelchair user), and Simpsons characters (Fox, now Disney). None can go into the app.

### 4.0 Ranked by value against effort

| Rank | Proposal | Value | Effort | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### 4b. Two new modes

**Picture-stimulus mode "An Spreagphictiúr"**

This is his five-finger method in Gerard's layout, with sentence-builder columns in place of the slashes.
1. **Roghnaigh.** Three stimulus cards side by side, each with a title and photo, and the pupil picks one. The card shows "Tóg do chuid ama" and an optional preparation countdown, off by default in line with the ADHD fork.
2. **An spreagadh.** The full card: photo, bold title, caption and at most 120 words of text. A five-finger hand runs along the bottom.
3. **Méar 1 go 5.** For each finger:
   - the question in Irish, spoken by the Abair voice;
   - the photo pinned at the top as a thumbnail;
   - a builder with chunk columns for that card, drawn from the phrase banks. Finger 1, for example: Sa lár / Ar thaobh na láimhe clé / Sa chúlra + feicim / feictear + ...

   The pupil says the sentence aloud and records it.
4. **Freagra samplach.** The model answer builds line by line beside the thumbnail, as in the decks. Alternatives appear as swap chips ("pictiúr / íomhá").
5. **Ceist nó dhó.** Two or three examiner follow-up questions. The pupil records and plays back.
6. **Dul siar.** The hand again, with a tick for each finger covered, plus the reassurance lines "Tá cead agat bheith ginearálta. Tá cead agat gan bheith i do shaineolaí." There are no percentages.

**Stimulus types.** Use more than photos, as the Tír Chonaill handout does: a short poem, graffiti, a protest placard, an advert, a list. Use your own photographs or licensed images, not clipart, press photos or celebrities. Protect the dignity of vulnerable people: the original homelessness photo hid the man's face.

Example card, about 85 words:

> **Loch nEathach: Ár Loch i mBaol**
> *(Photo: blue-green algae on the shore. Caption: Algaí gormghlasa ar chladach Loch nEathach, samhradh 2023.)*
> Is é Loch nEathach an loch is mó in Éirinn agus sa Bhreatain, agus is uaidh a thagann tuairim is 40% d'uisce óil an Tuaiscirt. Le cúpla samhradh anuas, áfach, tá an t-uisce clúdaithe le halgaí gormghlasa. Tugadh rabhadh do dhaoine gan dul ag snámh ann. Deir eolaithe gurb iad leasacháin feirme, séarachas agus aimsir níos teo is cúis leis an fhadhb. Cé air a bhfuil an locht? Agus cé a íocfaidh as?

Model answer, one line per finger:
1. Sa phictiúr seo feicim cladach locha agus tá an t-uisce glas agus tiubh.
2. Is pictiúr brónach é, dar liom, mar is léir go bhfuil an dúlra i mbaol.
3. Míníonn an téacs gur ón loch a thagann cuid mhór d'uisce óil an Tuaiscirt.
4. Is í an teachtaireacht atá ann nach féidir linn fanacht níos faide: caithfear gníomhú anois.
5. Chuaigh mé ag siúl cois an locha an samhradh seo caite agus bhí boladh bréan ón uisce. Sílim gur cheart do na feirmeoirí agus don Rialtas oibriú le chéile leis an loch a shábháil.

**Reading-passage mode "Léitheoireacht"**

This follows his worksheet layout and his six-task sequence, reordered so reading comes before production.
1. **An leathanach.**
   - A bold title that states a tension, using a colon.
   - A large photo with a caption in small italics beneath it.
   - Two to four paragraphs of at most 60 words each.
   - Target chunks underlined, as in the Australian football deck. A long press gives the English. An Abair audio button sits on each paragraph.
2. **Tuiscint.** (A) Answer in Irish, in your own words. The hint chips come from the text.
3. **Stór focal.** (B) Word-bank synonym gap-fill, then (a) a sentence of your own with a chunk.
4. **Gramadach.** (F) Cloze with bracketed cues, for example "(cosc: ginideach uatha)". Wrong answers are diagnosed by rule and linked through links.json to the grammar lesson.
5. **Athscríobh.** (C) Say it another way; (D) finish the sentence. Marked in the red pen for fadas and mutations.
6. **Aistriúchán.** (E) Exact English for one paragraph.
7. **Do bharúil.** A two-sided opinion builder ("Tá siad siúd atá i bhfábhar... / Ar an taobh eile den scéal... / Maidir liom féin...") that ends by linking to the matching Comhrá topic.

Sessions stay short, at five items by default.

Example passage, about 150 words, with the photo caption "Daltaí Bhliain 13: an chéad ghlúin gan toitín?":

> **An Ghlúin gan Toitín?**
> Nuair a cuireadh cosc ar chaitheamh tobac i dtithe tábhairne agus in oifigí sa Tuaisceart in 2007, bhí go leor daoine den bharúil nach n-oibreodh sé. Inniu, is beag duine a shamhlódh toitín a lasadh i mbialann.
> Tá an Rialtas anois ag iarraidh dul céim níos faide. Faoin dlí atá beartaithe, ní bheidh cead ag daoine a rugadh i 2009 nó ina dhiaidh sin tobac a cheannach choíche, fiú nuair a bheidh siad fásta. Tá siad siúd atá i bhfábhar an dlí ag rá go sábhálfaidh sé na mílte beatha agus na milliúin punt don tseirbhís sláinte.
> Ar an taobh eile den scéal, deir lucht cáinte an dlí gur ionsaí é ar shaoirse an duine. Má tá duine fásta sásta an baol a ghlacadh, cad chuige nach mbeadh sé de cheart aige a rogha féin a dhéanamh? Agus cad é faoi na toitíní leictreonacha, atá chomh coitianta sin i measc daoine óga?

Grammar cloze items:
- "Tá siad siúd atá i bhfábhar an ___ (cosc: ginideach uatha)" gives *choisc*.
- "___ (cuir: saorbhriathar, aimsir chaite) cosc ar thobac in 2007" gives *Cuireadh*.
- "daoine a ___ (beir: saorbhriathar, aimsir chaite) i 2009" gives *rugadh*.

### 4d. House style for teacher slides (his patterns, modernised)

1. **Irish first.** English appears only where a procedure must be exact, set smaller and in the muted grey (#8A8A8E), never mixed into the Irish line. This extends his two-column AS slide.
2. **One idea per slide.** The five fingers recur as a fixed spine across decks and app screens.
3. **The stimulus stays on screen.** The photo sits as a thumbnail while the model answer builds beside it, one line per click (his build reveal, which is also retrieval practice).
4. **Sentence-builder tables instead of slash strings.** Columns of chunks, with the target chunk in the single accent colour.
5. **Colour only where it means something**, under the house recolour test. His yellow becomes the chunk-to-learn accent. If colour is used for the five fingers, the same five colours must run through every deck and the app, with white text.
6. **Near-monochrome house palette.** Ink #15151A, maroon #6B1D2A as the one accent. Headings in Helvetica Neue Medium, no letter-spacing, no decorative bars. His dark Keynote ground suits photo and stimulus slides; text slides go light.
7. **Titles that state a tension**, with a colon or a question and never a dash: "An Dornálaíocht: spórt nó brúidiúlacht?"
8. **At most about 40 words of Irish per content slide** (his model-answer slides ran at 35 to 55). Reading passages go on handouts or in the app, not on a projected slide.
9. **Reassurance and a proverb as his signature.** Open with "Tóg do chuid ama"; close with "Cleachtadh a dhéanann máistreacht" or "Ní mar a shíltear a bhítear".
10. **No exam board, weightings, marks or band descriptors.** Success criteria in his own Irish: "Tuig. Tabhair tuairim. Tarraing ar do thaithí."
11. **Every fact carries its source and year** in a small grey line ("Fíric: NIHE, 2025"). No unsourced statistics.
12. **Own photographs or licensed images only.** No clipart, cartoons, celebrities or brand logos. Faces of vulnerable people are hidden.
