# GCSE group of legacy decks: analysis for Gan Ainm

## Summary

I analysed 18 files: 455 slides. Two of those files, the Millionaire template (167 slides) and a duplicate of one holidays deck (30 slides), are set aside, which leaves **258 content slides**. I also read the present-tense worksheet and the idiom grid.

- **Most of these decks are not Dáithí's own.** The metadata shows only the three 2009 Keynote files as his, plus a PowerPoint export of one of them. The other 14 files were made between 2003 and 2006 by colleagues, and one came from another school. The deck that best shows his method is **Laethanta Saoire Bliain 10 (2009)**. It works like this: the question is the heading, then a key-word table, then "spot the pattern", then "make a sentence", then "combine the answers", then a basic model answer, then "make it better", then homework and "learn it off by heart". Red marks the mutation.
- **Slide mix:** picture flashcards are 47% of slides, picture-only prompts 12%, question-led slides 10%, teacher guidance 7%, build-up and model-answer slides 6%, role plays 4% and grammar rules 4%.
- **Problems:** about 40 Irish errors. There is one ruling breach ("Dún" in the worksheet), dashes throughout, and "students". Some content is dated (birth years "naoi déag", "Air Hostess", "Housewife", typist, tape). Nearly all images are clipart or web photos. The Millionaire file carries a trademark and the show's theme music. One priest image is a racial caricature.
- **For the app:** the app already holds the 270 GCSE-level speaking questions and the GCSE word list, but it has no GCSE *builders*. Section 4 sets out a Year 11 track that Conor could use at home from next week. It has six units in order, each with a question chain, a sentence builder, one role play and a "make it better" ladder. Most of it can be built from the existing Year 8 and Comhrá AS rows.

### 1.2 The recurring patterns worth keeping

1. **The question is the headline.** The heading is the exam question in Irish ("Cá ndeachaigh tú ar do laethanta saoire?", "An réitíonn tú go maith le do theaghlach?"). The slide body answers it. 27 slides do this, and it is the organising principle of both of the strongest decks.
2. **Question chains.** Bliain 10 chains four questions: Cá ndeachaigh tú? Cad é mar a chuaigh tú? Cá fhad a d'fhan tú? Cá raibh tú ag stopadh? Pupils then "combine all three answers" and later "all four". Cuir síos ar dhaoine chains eight questions from name to personality, family and a favourite singer.
3. **Sentence frames with gaps.** Cuir síos gives openers such as "Is duine ___ ___ mé", "Deir mo chairde go bhfuil mé ___" and "Thig liom bheith ___ ó am go ham". It uses "a c(h)uid gruaige" and "a s(h)úile" so one frame serves both his and her. The app's builders already do this, and it is the direct ancestor of the builder format.
4. **Key words, then the mutated form.** Two-column tables: base form, then the form after the preposition (teach, i dteach; óstán, in óstán; bád, ar an bhád). **The mutation letter is in red.** Town.key does the same inside a compound (carr**chl**ós). Ionaid oibre does it in blue (i mbialann, san ionad sláinte).
5. **Grammar is found before it is told.** Pupils spot the pattern first: "Why does spelling change in some cases but not in others?" and "What happens when you put numbers before these words? Can you spot any patterns?" (lá, oíche, seachtain and mí after 1 to 6).
6. **A "make it better" ladder.** A basic model is followed by "Good answer, but basic! How could we make this answer better?". A table of alternative past-tense verbs follows (Thaistil mé, D'eitil mé, Thug mé cuairt ar, Chaith mé), then an improved model. The new phrases are in red, with "Can you work out the words in red?" and "Can you do better?".
7. **Success criteria and memorising.** "Where you went / How you got there / How long you stayed", then "Practice! Learn off by heart!". This is the same retrieval intent as the app's notebook.
8. **Role-play cards.** An overview card has the rubric in Irish and the tasks in English. Then there is one slide per task with 1 to 4 acceptable answers, graded from simple to rich ("Tá prátaí de dhíth orm fosta", then "Tá sé chomh maith dom prátaí a cheannach agus mé anseo"). The last slide is the full scripted dialogue.
9. **Grammar through a topic.** Gortuithe teaches the possessives mo, do, a and ár through body parts and injuries. It has labelled "his" and "her" body diagrams and two mnemonics: "Scallions smell spicy in stew" for sc, sm, sp and st not taking lenition, and "Many boys go campin' near ditches behind fences down town, nice girls back pack" for eclipsis. It ends with a translation task that has answers.
10. **Weather graded by feeling.** Laethanta saoire groups weather under Maith, Go measartha and Olc. This is a simple, good way to teach opinion.
11. **Assessment literacy (Speaking Test deck).** It describes what an A-grade answer does: three tenses, opinion plus justification, relative and purpose clauses, and "incidentals" (Ó am go ham, Caithfidh mé a rá, Cuireann sé isteach orm). It advises open questions, questions that link naturally, and discouraging long learned passages.

### 1.3 Typical sequence

- **Dáithí's own (Bliain 10):** title, then question 1 with key words, pattern question, question 2 and question 3, "make a sentence", "combine", basic model, improve, better model, homework and success criteria, "learn off by heart", then question 4 in the same cycle, "combine all four". There are about 4.6 reveal steps per slide: 139 build steps over 30 slides, all plain "appear" except one "swing". Nothing flies in.
- **Town.key (his):** 36 builds over 20 slides. The photo comes first and the word appears on click, which is retrieval by design.
- **Colleagues' decks:** title, then one flashcard per word. A question and answer list appears sometimes, an exercise rarely, and there is no build-up to a sentence.

### 1.4 Colour, type and density

- **His Keynote decks:** one typeface throughout (Gill Sans, 164 font references, nothing else in use). Deck 1 is a black-to-grey gradient with white text, yellow questions and italic yellow model answers, and red kept for mutations and new language. Town is plain white with a large photo and a lowercase label. The restraint is the lesson.
- **Colleagues' 2003 to 2006 decks:** full saturated backgrounds (red, pink, navy, yellow) in 5 decks and stock PowerPoint templates (blue ribbon, notebook, floral, gold key, ocean, dark swirl) in 6. Each bullet is in a different colour, titles are underlined like hyperlinks, and there is WordArt and cartoon clipart. Some slides cannot be read: dark blue title on black (Ollmhargadh), and 8 overlapping lines on the Mo Theaghlach cover.
- **Density:** flashcards carry 1 item. Question slides carry 3 to 8 bullets. The worst cases are 16 place names on one slide (Laethanta saoire), a 6 by 5 numbers table in tiny type (Bliain 10), and Sláinte agus SP set at about 12 point.

## 3. Problems

### 3.1 Irish errors (correct form in bold)

**Role plays**
- "Tá thainig siad isteach" → **Tháinig siad isteach ar maidin**
- "de dhiobhal orm" → **de dhíobháil orm**
- "Tá beirt dheartháireacha agam" → **beirt deartháireacha** (beirt does not lenite d, t or s. Year 8 b2 already has this right)
- "Tá mó dheartháir" → **mo**
- "Tá na seacht mbliana déag slánaithe aige" → **Tá seacht mbliana déag slánaithe aige**
- "Tá muid cúigear de theaghlach" is unidiomatic → **Tá cúigear againn sa teaghlach**
- The supermarket rubric says "Tá tú ag caint le do chara", but the teacher plays a shop assistant → **Tá tú san ollmhargadh. Labhraíonn an freastalaí ar dtús.**
- English typos: "vegtables" (twice), "qbout"

**Cuir síos ar dhaoine**
- "Cén bhliain inár rugadh thú?" → **inar** (inár means "in our")
- "Cén scoil ar a bhfuil tú?" → **Cén scoil a bhfuil tú ag freastal uirthi?**
- It mixes "Cé mhéad" and "Cá mhéad", and "thú" and "thu" → use **Cá mhéad** and **thú** throughout

**Laethanta Saoire Bliain 10** (Dáithí's)
- "D'fhán mé" (twice) → **D'fhan**
- "An Phortaingéal" → **An Phortaingéil**
- "Obair Baile" → **Obair bhaile**
- "Eochairfhocal" and "Eochairfhocail" are used inconsistently
- "cathair Bharcelona" → **cathair Barcelona** (foreign names are not normally lenited)

**Laethanta saoire (yellow)**

### 3.2 Ruling breaches

- **"Dún"** appears in the present-tense worksheet (set 1, item 9) → **Druid**.
- The Speaking Test deck uses "students" → **pupils**.
- Dashes: an en dash in the Mo Theaghlach title, and dashes throughout Slite beatha, Sláinte, the yellow Laethanta saoire, Gortuithe and the Speaking Test.
- No "-amar/-eamar", "clois" or "bord" appears in the group.
- **The app's own GCSE question bank** (data/questions_authored.json) has en dashes in Q080, Q149, Q169, Q176, Q188, Q266 and Q270. It also has "ab'fhearr" in Q111 and Q270 (→ **ab fhearr**) and "se" without a fada in Q240. Q188 mentions controlled assessment, which is out of date. These breaches will show up in any GCSE track.

### 3.3 Non-Ulster forms

The decks are strongly Ulster: Cad é mar, fosta, de dhíth, druid, Cá mhéad, thig liom, bunadh na háite, garach, galánta, ag cur fearthainne, ulpóg, sceadamán, déideadh, teach an phobail, ar an bhád, ar an fharraige, d'amharc mé. The only non-Ulster lapses are the synthetic "Éistfimid" and the mixing of "Cé mhéad" with "Cá mhéad".

### 3.4 Dated or unsuitable content

- Birth dates are framed as "naoi déag ___" (19xx). Year 11 pupils now were born in 2010 or 2011 (**dhá mhíle is a deich**).
- "Air Hostess" (keep aeróstach, gloss it "flight attendant"), "Bean an tí: Housewife", "Clóscríobhaí: Typist", "Siopa Ceirníní".
- "Ask your teacher to put it on tape", "Learning NI".
- The whole "presentation" component in the Speaking Test deck. The current GCSE speaking test is built on role plays and conversation; confirm against the current specification before publishing anything.
- One idiom in the Saibhreas grid ("ní fiú cac an mhadaidh bháin é") is not suitable for pupils.
- Gory images in Gortuithe (bleeding eyes, a car crash with blood) and caricatured faces in Sláinte and Slite beatha.

- **Clipart:** Sláinte, Éadach, Siopaí, both jobs decks and both role plays use Microsoft Office clipart, which was licensed for use inside Office documents. Do not move it into the app or a published page.
- **Town.key photos** are from the web: Belfast City Airport, Duke University Chapel (a US chapel used for séipéal), a US federal building, "Queens Café", a Spanish steel works (probably Wikimedia, which needs attribution) and a FreeFoto.com image with its watermark still on. ## 4. For the app in 2026

### 4.0 A GCSE track Conor can use at home from next week (lead item)

**What already exists and can be switched on today:** the notebook's 270 GCSE-level questions (12 pages, 189 Foundation and 81 Higher, each with an opener) and the GCSE word list in web/data/vocab.json. So from night one Conor can use the notebook filtered to Year 11 Foundation questions, one page at a time, in the order below. What is missing is a **GCSE builder file** (for example builders/gcse.json) and **role-play pages**. The six units below are that file. The first two can be built in a day from existing rows.

Every unit has the same five parts, taken straight from Dáithí's Bliain 10 sequence:
1. **Question chain:** 5 to 8 questions in order, Foundation first, linked to the notebook's question IDs.
2. **Sentence builder:** rows in the existing column format.
3. **Pattern box:** one mutation table, with the added letter in red, plus the "spot the pattern" question.
4. **Make it better:** three model answers (basic, better, best) with the new words in red.
5. **Role play:** a rubric in Irish, tasks in English, 2 acceptable answers per task, one unexpected question, then the full dialogue.

- Deir mo chairde go bhfuil mé | greannmhar / cairdiúil / cainteach / ciúin / spórtúil
- ach bím | leisciúil / crosta / cúthail | ó am go ham

Make it better:
- **Basic:** Tá cúigear i mo theaghlach.
- **Better:** Tá cúigear i mo theaghlach: mo mham, mo dhaidí, beirt deartháireacha agus mé féin.
- **Best:** Réitím go han-mhaith le mo dheartháir is óige mar is maith linn na rudaí céanna, ach caithfidh mé a rá go mbíonn muid ag argóint faoin teilifís ó am go ham.

Role play, *Cara nua*. Rubric: Tá tú ag caint le cara nua. Labhraíonn do chara ar dtús.
- Cá mhéad duine atá i do theaghlach? Answer: **Tá cúigear againn sa teaghlach.**
- An bhfuil deartháir ar bith agat? Answer: **Tá beirt deartháireacha agam.**
- An tusa an duine is sine? Answer: **Ní mé. Tá mo dheartháir níos sine ná mé.** (This teaches the copula answer.)
- Cén aois atá ag do dheartháir? Answer: **Tá sé seacht mbliana déag d'aois.**
- Unexpected: Cad é a dhéanann sibh le chéile? Answer: **Imríonn muid peil sa pháirc.**

The bridge in grammar terms:
- **Year 8:** the present, first person.
- **GCSE:** three tenses plus the analytic plural (chuaigh muid, beidh muid), mutation after prepositions and possessives, the copula answer, and opinion with a reason.
- **AS:** conditional, autonomous forms and connectors.

The "make it better" ladder is the same bridge inside a single unit.

### 4(c) House style for teacher slides, from his patterns

1. **The question is the title,** in Irish, exactly as the pupil will hear it.
2. **One idea per slide.** Flashcards show the picture first and bring in the word on the next click. Use plain appear builds only.
3. **Red means the mutation.** Reserve one accent colour for the added letter (i **d**teach, a **h**uillinn), and use it the same way in the slides, the app's red pen and printed sheets.
4. **Show nouns with the article** (an t-aerfort, an tsráid), never bare.
5. **Find the pattern, then state it:** a "spot the pattern" slide comes before the rule slide.
6. **Build to a paragraph:** word, then sentence, then combined answer, then basic model, then improved model with new words in red, then a success-criteria checklist, then learn it for the notebook.
7. **Role-play layout:** rubric in Irish, tasks in English, one task per slide with two graded answers, an unexpected question, and the full dialogue last.
8. **Irish on the slide, English in the presenter notes.** After the first term, use a fixed set of Irish instructions (Déan abairt; Cuir le chéile iad; An dtig leat níos fearr a dhéanamh?; Cad iad na focail dhearga?).
9. **Type and colour:** one sans typeface as he did (use the school's editorial deck system), a light and a dark theme, contrast that passes accessibility checks, no underlined titles, no WordArt, no dashes.
10. **Density:** at most 6 lines or a 6-row table, with pupil text at 28 point or larger. Split any table larger than that.
11. **Images:** use the school's own photographs of local places, or openly licensed images with credit. No clipart, no caricatures, no trademarked formats.
12. **Games:** a quiz ladder with lifelines works in class as a teacher-run game, built from scratch without the show's name or music. It does not belong in the app, which rules out scores and timers.
