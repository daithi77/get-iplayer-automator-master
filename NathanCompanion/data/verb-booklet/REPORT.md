# Your GCSE verb booklet: how you teach grammar, and what it means for Gan Ainm

28 September 2026. Source: *Ardscoil Naomh Pól, GCSE: Using Verbs* (six pages: cover, verb lists, key terms, the master grid, syncopated verbs, the irregular verbs). Every page was read, including the colour tables.

## 1. What the booklet is

It is a **reference tool, not a workbook**. There are no exercises in it. It gives a pupil everything needed to produce any verb form in four tenses on five pages, and it is built to be looked up in the middle of writing or speaking. The practice happened in your classroom; the booklet made the practice possible.

It moves in a deliberate order:

1. **Page 1: sort the verbs.** About 110 common verbs in colour-coded boxes: 1st conjugation broad (orange), 1st slender (yellow), 2nd broad (green), 2nd slender (blue), syncopated (purple), irregular (pink, numbered 1 to 11).
2. **Page 2: the two moves.** Aspirate (h after the first letter) and eclipse (a letter in front), each defined in one plain sentence, with the letters that change, the letters that never change, and a mnemonic for each list.
3. **Page 3: one grid for everything.** Four tenses across the top (past, present, future, conditional, each with 1st and 2nd conjugation), and down the side: positive for verbs that can be aspirated, positive for vowel verbs, positive for f-verbs, then negative, question and autonomous. Every cell is a short numbered procedure.
4. **Page 4: the exception with its own page.** Syncopated verbs, with one sentence of rule and a seven-verb table across all four tenses, each tense its own colour.
5. **Page 5: the irregulars, verb by verb.** Eleven verbs, each a colour band, each with P, N and Q rows across all four tenses.

## 2. Your style of teaching grammar, deduced

These are the principles the booklet runs on. Each is backed by what is on the page.

1. **Classify before you conjugate.** The first thing a pupil meets is not a rule but a sorting task: which box is this verb in? Conjugation (1st or 2nd), then broad or slender, then the two special boxes (syncopated, irregular). Everything after page 1 assumes the pupil knows the box.
2. **Broad or slender is the decision point, every time.** Nearly every cell of the master grid begins "If broad ... If slender ...". The pupil asks one question and the ending follows.
3. **Rules are procedures, not descriptions.** Each cell is a numbered list of moves in order: "1. If broad, remove -aigh, aspirate and add: -óinn / -óimis / -óidís". A small set of named moves does all the work: aspirate, prefix d', remove -aigh or -igh, add the ending, add the pronoun, add Ní / An / Níor / Ar, remove d'.
4. **The same moves in every tense.** Because the grid sets the tenses side by side, the pupil sees that the negative is always "add Ní and aspirate" and the question always "add An and eclipse", except in the past (Níor, Ar). The pattern across tenses is the lesson.
5. **Positive, negative and question always travel together.** On page 3 they are consecutive rows; on page 5 every irregular verb has its P, N and Q rows side by side in every tense. The autonomous form sits in the same grid, not in a separate chapter.
6. **The exceptions get named and boxed.** Vowel verbs, f-verbs, the s* and f* caveats, syncopated verbs and the eleven irregulars each have a labelled row, box or page, so a pupil knows when the normal rule does not apply.
7. **Three forms, not six.** The irregular table groups the persons as I / you, he, she, you plural, they / we, with the persons colour-coded in the heading. A pupil learns the mé form, the form for everyone else, and the we form.
8. **Mnemonics for anything that must be memorised, and they are funny.** "Britney caught dopey friends going mad publicly, silly twits" (the letters that take h); "He likes noodles and rice" (h, l, n, r never do); "Scallions smell spicy in stew" (sc, sm, sp, st); "Many boys go camping near ditches behind fences, while nice girls buy pens down town" (the eclipsis pairs).
9. **Colour carries meaning, consistently.** Blue for the original letter, red for the letter added. Each verb group has its colour; each tense has its colour on the syncopated page; each irregular verb has its own band. The colour is a map, not decoration.
10. **English explains, Irish names.** Headings pair the Irish term with the English one (An Aimsir Chaite, Past Tense). Instructions are in plain English. The grammatical terms are the traditional ones (aspirate, eclipse, conjugation, syncopated, autonomous), each defined once.
11. **Examples are things you would say to a person, often with a wink.** "An bpósfaidh tú mé?", "An dtiocfaidh tú chuig an dioscó liom?", "An mbuailfimid ag an chaife?", "i nDún Dealgan". Questions to "tú", everyday situations, local places.
12. **Everything on one page where possible.** The master grid fits four tenses and six forms on a single landscape page. The pupil can hold the whole system in one look.

## 3. What needs updating for 2026

**Against your rulings**

- **First person plural.** The booklet teaches only the synthetic forms (-aimid, -faimid, -óimid). Your ruling for the app is the analytic form first (cuireann muid, cuirfidh muid), with both accepted. This also simplifies principle 7: in the present and future, "we" joins "everyone else" (cuireann sé, cuireann muid).
- **Conditional, we and they.** The booklet gives -faimis / -faidís only. Your ruling shows both: chuirfimis or chuirfeadh muid, chuirfidís or chuirfeadh siad.
- **Clois.** The irregular table is headed CLOIS. The app uses cluin (cluinim, chuala, cluinfidh).
- **Ulster forms.** The irregulars are standard only. The app accepts the Ulster forms: gheibhim (faigh), tchím (feic), bhéarfaidh (tabhair), níor dhúirt (abair).

**Errors on the pages**

- Page 3: the conditional endings -faimís and -fimís should be -faimis and -fimis (no fada).
- Page 3: the present autonomous for 2nd conjugation slender reads "-tear"; it should be "remove -igh and add -ítear". "an add" should be "and add" (three places).
- Page 5: Ní bhuair mé → **Ní bhfuair mé**; Ní chloifimid → Ní chloisfimid; Ní thabharfadh (future) → **Ní thabharfaidh**; Tabharfainn → **Thabharfainn**; An ndéardfá → **An ndéarfá**; An ndeir / ndeir* → **An ndeirim / ndeir**; An mbearfá → **An mbéarfá**; An ndeanann → **An ndéanann**; D'fheicimis and Ní fheicimis → **d'fheicfimis**, **fheicfimis**; An bhfeicimid (future) → **An bhfeicfimid**; An bhfaigheá? / → the stray question mark.
- Page 1: inis appears both as 1st conjugation slender and as syncopated; neartaigh appears twice; "to practice" should be "to practise".
- Page 4: "the last two vowels are removed" is loose: it is the vowel or vowels of the last syllable (oscail → oscl-, imir → imr-).

**Terminology.** "Aspirate" is your word and the one your pupils knew. The app says séimhiú and urú. I recommend the Irish term with your definition beside it: "séimhiú (aspirate: put h after the first letter)", "urú (eclipse: put a letter in front)". Your mnemonics work unchanged.

## 4. Where the app departs from your style, ranked by impact

1. **No classification step.** The app's grammar is organised by tense, then by group (Grúpa 1, Grúpa 2, irregular). It never asks the pupil "which box is this verb in?". Your whole booklet starts there.
2. **Positive, negative and question are split up.** In the present tense, for example, the negative and the question are separate lessons from the positive ("Ceist le An, diúltach le Ní", "Briathra a thosaíonn ar ghuta"). You always show the three together.
3. **No view across tenses.** The app teaches each tense in its own section. Nothing shows a pupil that the same moves repeat across all four tenses, which is the point of your master grid.
4. **Irregular verbs are taught tense by tense.** The app has "Beir, an aimsir láithreach", "Beir, an aimsir fháistineach" and so on, in different sections. You teach each irregular verb as one card across all tenses.
5. **Rules are written as prose.** The app's rules are good sentences, but they are not your numbered procedures of named moves.
6. **No mnemonics.** None of your four appears anywhere in the app.
7. **No colour map for verb groups or tenses.** The app uses red for changed letters (as you do) and green for particles, but verb groups and tenses have no colours.
8. **Examples are sound but rarely playful.** They are mostly third-person statements about school life; yours are questions to a person, with humour.

## 5. The design rules for every grammar asset from now on

Written into `GRAMMAR-STYLE.md` at the root of the project, so every lesson, practice set and slide deck, and every agent that drafts one, follows them.

1. **Box first.** Every verb activity starts by placing the verb in its box, with the box colour: 1st broad orange, 1st slender yellow, 2nd broad green, 2nd slender blue, syncopated purple, irregular pink.
2. **Broad or slender asked every time.** The first step of every regular-verb procedure is that question.
3. **Rules as numbered moves.** Every rule is written as two to four numbered steps using the named moves: séimhiú (aspirate), urú (eclipse), prefix d', remove -aigh / -igh, add the ending, add Ní / An / Níor / Ar.
4. **P, N and Q together.** Every lesson, table, worked example and slide shows positive, negative and question as a set; the autonomous form sits alongside, not apart.
5. **A cross-tense overview.** Each verb group has one overview (your master grid, updated) that the lessons link back to.
6. **Two forms plus we.** Teach the mé form and the form for everyone else, with muid (analytic) as the default we-form and the synthetic form shown beside it; the conditional shows both forms for we and they.
7. **Irregulars as verb cards.** Eleven cards in your order (beir, ith, tar, cluin, tabhair, faigh, abair, déan, téigh, feic, bí), each with P, N and Q across the four tenses, standard and Ulster forms both shown.
8. **Your mnemonics, word for word,** wherever séimhiú, the letters that resist it, and urú are taught.
9. **Colour means one thing.** Red for the letter added (with underline), green for the particle, and the fixed box and tense colours. Never colour alone: always underline or label too.
10. **English explains, Irish names.** Every heading pairs the Irish term with the English one; explanations are in plain English.
11. **Examples you would say to someone.** Prefer questions to tú, real situations and local places, with humour where it fits.
12. **One screen, the whole pattern.** An overview must fit on one screen or one slide.

## 6. Decisions I took

- The report and the style rules are written; I have not yet changed existing lessons. Rules 1 to 12 apply to every grammar asset built from here, and the retrofit of the existing 40 sections is ranked in section 4.
- The booklet's own errors are listed, not silently copied.
- The PDF itself is not added to the repository (it carries the school crest); this report quotes it.
