Dáithí Murray, with Claude. 26 September 2026, second version: the whole school, Years 8 to 12. Supersedes the first version of the same day (kept in data/concept-panel-2026-09-26.json and data/whole-school-2026-09-26.json), which was written for Years 11 and 12 only and was rejected on that ground.

## How this was arrived at

Five concepts from five angles were judged by a Year 10 pupil, the head of Irish and a product designer; all three chose the pupil's own exam script. Dáithí ruled that the app must serve the whole school. Three strategies for that were then developed and judged by a Year 8 pupil, the head of Irish and a designer: one booklet with graded lines, two booklets in one shape, and the conversation rather than the exam as the thing prepared for. The Year 8 and the designer chose the conversation; the head of Irish chose the graded booklet. The brief below is one booklet whose date is the next real conversation, with the exam as the last of them. It was attacked by the same three judges and revised.

# Mo Scríbhinn (working title)

## What it is for

Write what you will say in your next conversation in Irish, one line a night, until nothing is new.

After twenty school nights: sheet 1a in biro and rewritten from memory, sheet 2a half filled, one card sent to the teacher, the date of its conversation on every screen.

## The thread

The script. One booklet belonging to one pupil from Year 8 to the exam in Year 12: twelve topic pages cut into sheets by tier, a sheet being one page's questions at one tier, in order, the same cut for every pupil, so 1a.4 is Q004 for a Year 8 and the candidate she becomes. A line is the pupil's own answer to one question, in biro, marked in the Principal's red hand for what a machine can check, sent to the teacher who corrects the rest, rewritten from memory until it is theirs.

The tier is the year a question first opens (1 in Year 8, 2 in Year 9, 3 in Year 10, exam-only in Year 11) and the grade follows the tier, not the year: Abairt amháin on tier 1 in every year; le ham nó áit on tier 2; Dhá abairt, agus cad chuige on tier 3, Freagra iomlán from Year 11. The date in the candidate box (Ainm, Rang, Líne 7 de 12, never a score) is the next conversation with a fluent speaker (a teacher, a Year 12 partner, the examiner last); the department's calendar fills it, so it is never blank. A line is crossed through and dated only when a new draft is written over it, one more sentence in each year, so a Year 8 line sits under the Year 12 answer.

## First open

The inside cover: Ainm; Bliain 8 to 12 with the year's rule under it; Rang (8B); the next conversation from the calendar; the year's pages, fixed for Years 8 to 10, chosen in Year 11 from the twelve; townland from the catchment list with its form after Tá mé i mo chónaí; club after Imrím le; the school's name; the teacher's address. Then sheet 1a, blank, its eight questions on the rules. The text is permanent; the fields are not.

Irish: Seo do scríbhinn féin. Ar na leathanaigh seo tá na ceisteanna a chuirfeas cainteoir Gaeilge ort sa chomhrá, agus do chuid freagraí féin orthu. Achan oíche: líne amháin nua i do chuid focal féin, agus líne a scríobh tú cheana, ó chuimhne. Ceartóidh an peann dearg na fadaí, an séimhiú agus an t-urú; is í an aip a mharcálann, i lámh an Phríomhoide. Do mhúinteoir a cheartóidh an chuid eile, ón leathanach a sheolann tú. Faoin chéad chomhrá eile ní bheidh rud ar bith le rá agat den chéad uair.

English beneath, line for line, opening: This is your own script: what you will say.

## Home

No home apart from the work: the app opens on Anocht, rubric Do chomhrá, scríofa agat roimh ré. Night one is one rule at a time: in Year 8, Líon an bhearna, Q001, Q002, Q006 and Q007 framed in grey from the cover and copied in biro, then Q003 to Q005 with the personal numerals in the word bank; in later years the same seven from memory, falling due one a night.

## The screens

**1. Anocht.** Four items, fixed order, one live, never fewer. 1 Ó chuimhne: the most overdue line rewritten from memory, marked only on listed words missing; Abair é beneath it from box 2, unmarked until Abair. After a conversation, An comhrá: the partner's ticks copied from the card. 2 Focal: a word missed in item 1, else a listed word from the next question, else the next unused headword in band order, in the pupil's own sentence. 3 Foirm: the pupil's own line turned: Aimsir, another tense; Bearna, sa ______ (carr); An freagra eile, the other answer to a yes or no question; Ceist, the line's question from memory, the person shifted. 4 Líne nua, the next open question, or when none remains Líne níos fearr, the oldest line given one more sentence as a dated draft. The pen writes at most three things, glossed in grey English: fada!; a mutation note after mo, do, i, or preposition and article where the gender is known; cad chuige? on a one-sentence opinion without a reason word; bheith ag after is maith liom ag. The opener in grey on the rule; Fág go fóill twice, then Athscríobh le cuidiú; the stamp: date, one kind line, Sin é anocht.

**2. An scríbhinn.** The year's pages and open sheets, nothing grey; from Year 11 the twelve with counts. Stamps in the hand: scríofa, ó chuimhne, ticeáilte, Críochnaithe. Foot action Seol chuig an mhúinteoir: the sheet as an A4 card through the share sheet, printed by the department once a half term.

**3. Líne.** The question as rubric; drafts crossed through and dated; word bank; Athscríobh ó chuimhne; Ceartú an mhúinteora as the key.

**4. Innéacs.** Words already in a line, by sheet, with gender and line reference; Mo chuid focal féin; Le ceartú, five entries, crossed through when written right.

**5. Gan do scríbhinn.** From a sheet that is scríofa, three or five questions in print, record, reveal, playback, tic or arís; the countdown from Year 11; Scrúdú béil by name only once Abair speaks.

**6. An clúdach istigh.** Details, conversations, the text, Priontáil an scríbhinn ar fad, Cóip den scríbhinn, Oscail cóip, Glan an bhliain seo behind the typed name.

## The years

Shared: all above; boxes of 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 days, advanced by rewriting to box 3 and past it only by a card tick; page 1, Mé féin, on every year's calendar; one Script file in iCloud across five years.

What differs:

- Year 8: tier 1; by default Mé féin and School in the first half term, Free time in the second and third, Local area and Social media after; Foirm is An freagra eile and Ceist; the pen writes fada! and mutation notes only; three questions on Gan do scríbhinn; the first conversation is with Bliain 12.
- Year 9: tier 2 on the same pages; Foirm adds Aimsir and Bearna; five questions.
- Year 10: tier 3 on the same pages, plus Culture and Extra-curricular; cad chuige? and bheith ag begin; the opener waits twenty seconds.
- Year 11: two pages from twelve beside page 1; Scrúdú, Bealtaine 2028 in the date cell; the countdown; the pace line above one line a night.
- Year 12: as Year 11 with Bealtaine 2027; two cards a half term if the department asks.

## The exam-script register, what it must do

Every screen must pass as a page of a candidate's script, or as the partner's card. The pen writes only what a teacher could check; the card carries the question in print, the pupil's line, a tick box for the partner, a blank rule for the teacher and Mo cheist at the foot.

## For a pupil with ADHD

Progress is an object that fills: a sheet, scríofa inside a fortnight, Críochnaithe inside the half term. The same four items in the same order every night, one live; the queue never shown. Marking is instant and glossed; one wrong form is written out again before the night closes. Le ceartú holds five things and empties. No streak, no timer until Year 11.

## What comes out of the current build

The TabView; HomeView's statistics and streak; the score slot in ExamHeader; GrammarView; SettingsView's Form and stage picker; intervals 0, 1, 3, 7, 14, 30; per-word ticks; the Audio folder; Years 11 and 12 only; Ceist ar ais; Mo chuid botún; the printed-sheet gate; the twenty-second wait and grey pages below Year 10; the name Cuimhne.

## What has to be true before it can be built

Data: the topic table; the 270 questions with gloss, tier, exam-only flag, sameAs, register, pair and opener; the department's calendar of pages and conversation dates; the school's name in Irish; the catchment townlands with locatives; the verb paradigm table; a gender supplement for Year 8 words; every Irish string signed off by the Principal. Audio: none until Abair. One booklet, Years 8 to 12, in iCloud; phonics and the real conversation are the classroom's.
