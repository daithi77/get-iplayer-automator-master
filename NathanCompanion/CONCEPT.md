Dáithí Murray, with Claude. 26 September 2026. Supersedes the module list in PROPOSAL.md section 3. Decision pending on the three questions at the end.
## How this was arrived at

After the second proof of concept was rejected ("not clear what the point of the app is; nothing running through the app explaining what it is for"), five product concepts were developed independently from five angles: the pupil's own exam script; five minutes a night for a pupil with ADHD; the teacher's red pen at home; the road from Year 8 to Year 12; the pupil's own Irish voice. Three judges scored them: a Year 10 pupil, the head of Irish, a product designer. All three chose the exam script, with the five-minute concept second. The brief below was written from the winner, grafted with the best of the others, attacked by the same three judges, and revised to answer them.

# Mo Scríbhinn (working title)

## What it is for

Write your own answers to the examiner's questions, one line a night, until nothing in the conversation is new.

After twenty school nights: twenty lines in biro on the two chosen pages, the first sheet finished, each line rewritten from memory and said aloud, one printed sheet back from the teacher with a correction typed in.

## The thread

The script. One exam booklet belonging to one candidate, and it is the data structure, not a skin: the candidate's two conversation topics as pages, each cut into sheets of ten to twenty numbered lines. A line is the pupil's own answer to one question, in biro, marked in the Principal's red hand for what a machine can check, printed for the teacher who corrects the rest, rewritten and later said from memory until it is theirs.

On every screen: the candidate box carries Ainm, Rang, Scrúdú (Bealtaine 2028) and the standing on the current sheet (Líne 5 de 11), never a score; the italic rubric with its English beneath is the only heading; each item has a print instruction; a rule carries only biro; the margin carries line references in print and marks in the hand; one action at the foot, a grey running foot naming the other pages; no tab bar.

## First open

The inside cover, filled in as a candidate fills a real one: Ainm, Rang (11 or 12), Scrúdú, two topic pages ticked, townland as said after Tá mé i mo chónaí, club as said after Imrím le. Then the first sheet, blank, its questions printed on the rules: the object before the paragraph. The cover's text is permanent.

Irish: Seo do scríbhinn féin. Ar na leathanaigh seo tá na ceisteanna a chuirfeas an scrúdaitheoir ort sa chomhrá, agus do chuid freagraí féin orthu. Achan oíche: líne amháin nua i do chuid focal féin, agus líne a scríobh tú cheana, ó chuimhne. Ceartóidh an peann dearg na fadaí, an séimhiú agus an t-urú; is í an aip a mharcálann, i lámh an Phríomhoide. Do mhúinteoir a cheartóidh an chuid eile, ón leathanach a phriontálann tú. Faoi lá an scrúdaithe ní bheidh rud ar bith le rá agat den chéad uair.

English: This is your own exam script. On these pages are the questions the examiner will ask you in the conversation, and your own answers to them. Every night: one new line in your own words, and one line you wrote before, from memory. The red pen corrects fadas, séimhiú and urú; the app does the marking, in the Principal's hand. Your teacher corrects the rest, from the page you print. By exam day there will be nothing you have to say for the first time.

## Home

No home apart from the work: the app opens on Anocht; leathanach means a topic page only. Every night the rubric reads Do scrúdú, scríofa agat roimh ré, English beneath; then the standing and item 1 live. The one action: write on the live rule. Night one has one item: the seven one-sentence questions at the top of the first sheet, written together.

## The screens

**1. Anocht.** Four items, fixed order, one live. 1 Ó chuimhne, the most overdue line, never two: in box 0 or 1 Athscríobh, from memory, marked only on listed words missing from the saved line; from box 2 Abair é, sheet face down, recorded, played back against the revealed line. 2 Focal: a word missed in item 1, else a listed word from the next question, in the pupil's own sentence. 3 Foirm: a gap in the pupil's own line, Téim ar scoil sa ______ (carr), diagnosed by rule. 4 Líne nua: the next question. The pen writes at most three things: fada!, a mutation note where the noun's gender is known, cad chuige? on an opinion question without a reason word, bheith ag after a bare is maith liom ag; never per-word ticks or question marks. The opener in grey print after twenty seconds idle; Fág go fóill skips. Ceartúcháin: one form, once. Close: the date, one kind line about one thing, Sin é anocht.

**2. An scríbhinn.** Rubric: Gach a ndéarfaidh tú ar lá an scrúdaithe. The two ticked pages and their sheets, the rest grey; lines in biro with date, taifeadta, le seiceáil until printed; Críochnaithe across a finished sheet; Priontáil an leathanach; Scrúdú béil once finished.

**3. Líne.** Rubric: the question. Drafts crossed through and dated, recordings, word bank, Athscríobh ó chuimhne, Ceartú an mhúinteora: the teacher's correction typed in becomes the key, one tap naming the fault.

**4. Innéacs.** Rubric: Na focail atá i do scríbhinn. Only words already in a line, by sheet, gender where known, line reference; then Mo chuid focal féin and Mo chuid botún in the hand.

**5. Scrúdú béil.** Rubric: Cúig cheist, gan do scríbhinn os do chomhair. From a finished sheet, pairs in sequence, the question in the teacher's voice, print on a second tap, the only countdown, tic or arís.

**6. An clúdach istigh.** Rubric: Cé thú féin agus cá huair a bheas an scrúdú. The candidate details, the text above, a print line that the exam is a conversation on two chosen topics, Priontáil an scríbhinn ar fad, Glan.

## A school night in five minutes

Aoife, 11B, sheet 1b, Líne 5 de 11. Item 1, Athscríobh 1a.4, Cá mhéad deartháir atá agat?: she leaves out amháin; the pen writes amháin de dhíth. Item 2: amháin in her own sentence, tic. Item 3, sa ______ (carr) from her own 1a.2: she types carr; crossed, charr above, séimhiú de dhíth. Item 4, Cad é mar a réitíonn tú le do chlann?: Réitím go maith le mo chlann. Is maith liom ag caint le mo mhamaí mar tá sí greannmhar. The pen writes bheith ag caint. Ceartúcháin: sa charr once. The stamp: the date, sa charr ceartaithe, maith thú, Sin é anocht.

## The exam-script register, what it must do

Keep it. The test for every screen: could it be printed and handed to a teacher as a page of a candidate's script. The pen writes only what a teacher could check and would write; the printed sheet carries a blank rule under each line and a tick box for the teacher's real red pen. It fails the moment the pen ticks what it cannot judge.

## For a pupil with ADHD

Progress is an object that fills inside a fortnight: a sheet, Críochnaithe across it. The same four items in the same order every night, one live; the queue never shown; the count is the current sheet. Marking is instant; one wrong form is written out again before the night closes. Boxes of 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 days, a line moving from paper to voice after two right rewrites. No streak, no timer except the cold exam.

## What comes out of the current build

The TabView. HomeView's statistics, streak and maith tick. The score slot in ExamHeader. The Aonad labels. GrammarView, its rule box, féach an riail and the gender default of m. SettingsView's Form and stage picker. Intervals 0, 1, 3, 7, 14, 30. From the last brief: per-word ticks and the grey ?, odd and even pages, Focal from the whole list, the tally in the stamp, leathanach for a night, Years 8 to 10. The name Cuimhne.

## What has to be true before it can be built

Data: a twelve-row topic table with Irish page titles, vocabulary groups and sheet cuts; the 270 questions each with English gloss, tier, register, follow-up pair and a three to five word opener, the 42 slash questions resolved, Q093 and Q094 authored; the topic list's gender cleaned in the workbook, until which Foirm drills the article only on the 362 gendered frequency-list nouns; an apostrophe and capital fadas digitised from the sample sheet; every Irish string signed off by a named teacher. Audio: the 270 questions recorded by a teacher on a phone before the cold exam may claim to be like the day. Model answers: none; the pupil's line is the key, the teacher's correction replaces it, the openers stand in. Years 11 and 12 only; Key Stage 3 is a second booklet.

## Ideas grafted from the losing concepts

- From the dictionary page (the rejected canvas row): the Innéacs as a printed word list per sheet with gender after nouns and a line reference, and the tap-to-add personal word list; listed-word recognition lives in the index, not in the pen.
- From PROPOSAL direction B (rule-based grammar engine): kept, but as Foirm inside the pupil's own line in the cloze shape of the ExamGrammar canvas (Chuaigh mé abhaile sa ______ (carr)), not an endless drill with a score.
- From PROPOSAL direction D (sentence scaffolds): the word bank's first row is the frames is maith liom + ainm briathartha, ba mhaith liom + ..., is dóigh liom go ..., and the pen's bheith ag note is the engine applying the consequence of the frame.
- From PROPOSAL direction C (speaking rehearsal with a personal answer bank): the answer bank becomes the script itself and the cold exam its test; recording is kept only where the pupil hears it back against their line.
- From PROPOSAL direction E (teacher view): no screen; the teacher's view is the printed sheet with a blank rule and tick box per line, and the return leg is Ceartú an mhúinteora typed in by the pupil.
- From the pupil critique: sheets of ten to twenty lines so a page fills inside a fortnight; Anocht as the nightly unit with leathanach reserved for the topic page; teacher-recorded questions before Abair; the intro as the permanent inside cover text.
- From the Head of Irish critique: the box schedule moving a line from paper to voice; follow-up question pairs asked in sequence; Focal drawn from the next question's own words; the is maith liom ag diagnosis; t before s in Ulster after preposition and article; the first open as the filled cover.
- From the product designer critique: night one as the seven short questions written together; 270 openers instead of model answers; the pen limited to three checkable things with no per-word marks; the index listing only words already in a line; the stamp naming the work instead of counting it; the running foot carrying navigation so the foot keeps one action.
