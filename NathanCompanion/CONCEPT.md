## How this was arrived at

Five concepts from five angles were judged by a Year 10 pupil, the head of Irish and a product designer; all three chose the pupil's own exam script. Dáithí ruled that the app must serve the whole school. Three strategies for that were then developed and judged by a Year 8 pupil, the head of Irish and a designer: one booklet with graded lines, two booklets in one shape, and the conversation rather than the exam as the thing prepared for. The Year 8 and the designer chose the conversation; the head of Irish chose the graded booklet. # Mo Scríbhinn (working title)

## What it is for

Write what you will say in your next conversation in Irish, one line a night, until nothing is new.

After twenty school nights: sheet 1a in biro and rewritten from memory, sheet 2a half filled, sheet 1a sent to the teacher, the day of the next Irish class on every screen, the first lines ticked ráite the night after a class.

## The thread

The script. One booklet belonging to one pupil from Year 8 to the exam in Year 12: twelve topic pages cut into sheets by tier, a sheet being one page's questions at one tier, in order, the same cut for every pupil, so 1a.4 is Q004 for a Year 8 and the candidate she becomes. A line is the pupil's own answer to one question, in biro, marked in the Principal's red hand for what a machine can check, sent to the teacher who corrects the rest in their own time, rewritten from memory until it is theirs.

The tier is the year a question first opens (1 in Year 8, 2 in Year 9, 3 in Year 10, exam-only in Year 11) and the grade follows the tier, not the year: Abairt amháin on tier 1 in every year; le ham nó áit on tier 2; Dhá abairt, agus cad chuige on tier 3, Freagra iomlán from Year 11. The date in the candidate box (Ainm, Rang, Líne 7 de 12, never a score) is the next time the pupil will be asked in Irish by someone fluent: for Years 8 to 10 the next Irish class on the timetable, where the teacher opens with the sheet's questions and pair work asks the rest, walked from the class days ticked once on the cover and the school's term dates; for Years 11 and 12 the exam month, the examiner last; so it is never blank and nothing is staged for it. A line is crossed through and dated only when a new draft is written over it, one more sentence in each year, so a Year 8 line sits under the Year 12 answer.

## First open

The inside cover: Ainm; Bliain 8 to 12 with the year's rule under it; Rang (8B); the days with an Irish class, Luan to Aoine, a second row and which week this is when the timetable runs on two weeks; the year's pages, fixed for Years 8 to 10, chosen in Year 11 from the twelve; townland from the catchment list with its form after Tá mé i mo chónaí; club after Imrím le; the school's name; the teacher's address. Then sheet 1a, blank, its eight questions on the rules. The text is permanent; the fields are not.

Irish: Seo do scríbhinn féin. Ar na leathanaigh seo tá na ceisteanna a chuirfeas cainteoir Gaeilge ort sa chomhrá, agus do chuid freagraí féin orthu. Achan oíche: líne amháin nua i do chuid focal féin, agus líne a scríobh tú cheana, ó chuimhne. Ceartóidh an peann dearg na fadaí, an séimhiú agus an t-urú; is í an aip a mharcálann, i lámh an Phríomhoide. Do mhúinteoir a cheartóidh an chuid eile, ón leathanach a sheolann tú. Is é an chéad rang Gaeilge eile do chéad chomhrá eile, nuair a chuirfeas do mhúinteoir ceist ort; an scrúdaitheoir an duine deireanach a chuirfeas iad. Faoin chéad chomhrá eile ní bheidh rud ar bith le rá agat den chéad uair.

English beneath, line for line, opening: This is your own script: what you will say. The added sentence: Your next conversation is the next Irish class, when your teacher asks you a question; the examiner is the last who will ask them. Dáithí signs off the added sentence.

## Home

No home apart from the work: the app opens on Anocht, rubric Do chomhrá, scríofa agat roimh ré. Night one is one rule at a time: in Year 8, Líon an bhearna, Q001, Q002, Q006 and Q007 framed in grey from the cover and copied in biro, then Q003 to Q005 with the personal numerals in the word bank; in later years the same seven from memory, falling due one a night.

## The screens

**1. Anocht.** Four items, fixed order, one live, never fewer. 1 Ó chuimhne: the most overdue line rewritten from memory, marked only on listed words missing; Abair é beneath it from box 2, unmarked until Abair; on the night after an Irish class, when the line is at box 3, one question beneath Abair é once it has played back: Ar dhúirt tú í sa rang inniu?, a tick box in the pupil's biro, dated with the class, never in the hand. 2 Focal: a word missed in item 1, else a listed word from the next question, else the next unused headword in band order, in the pupil's own sentence. 3 Foirm: the pupil's own line turned: Aimsir, another tense; Bearna, sa ______ (carr); An freagra eile, the other answer to a yes or no question; Ceist, the line's question from memory, the person shifted. 4 Líne nua, the next open question, or when none remains Líne níos fearr, the oldest line given one more sentence as a dated draft. The pen writes at most three things, glossed in grey English: fada!; a mutation note after mo, do, i, or preposition and article where the gender is known; cad chuige? on a one-sentence opinion without a reason word; bheith ag after is maith liom ag. The opener in grey on the rule; Fág go fóill twice, then Athscríobh le cuidiú; the stamp: date, one kind line, Sin é anocht.

**2. An scríbhinn.** The year's pages and open sheets, nothing grey; from Year 11 the twelve with counts. Stamps in the hand: scríofa, ó chuimhne, Críochnaithe, seolta, ceartaithe; ráite with its class date in biro beside each line. Foot action Seol chuig an mhúinteoir: the sheet as an A4 card through the share sheet to the address on the cover, offered the night the sheet is scríofa and never by a date; then Ceartú ar ais: from the card the teacher returns whenever they like, Ceart mar atá or the teacher's line, entered line by line, a line with no mark left as it is.

**3. Líne.** The question as rubric; drafts crossed through and dated; word bank; Athscríobh ó chuimhne; Ceartú an mhúinteora as the key.

**4. Innéacs.** Words already in a line, by sheet, with gender and line reference; Mo chuid focal féin; Le ceartú, five entries, crossed through when written right.

**5. Gan do scríbhinn.** From a sheet that is scríofa, three or five questions in print, record, reveal, playback, tic or arís, tic moving no box until Abair; the countdown from Year 11; Scrúdú béil by name only once Abair speaks.

**6. An clúdach istigh.** Details, the Irish class days, the text, Priontáil an scríbhinn ar fad, Cóip den scríbhinn, Oscail cóip, Glan an bhliain seo behind the typed name.

## The years

Shared: all above; boxes of 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 days, advanced by rewriting to box 3, to box 4 by the pupil's own ráite tick the night after a class and a right rewrite, to box 5 by the teacher's reading of the line entered from the returned sheet and a right rewrite, a line whose teacher never replies stopping at box 4; page 1, Mé féin, on every year's calendar; one Script file in iCloud across five years.

What differs:

- Year 8: tier 1; by default Mé féin and School in the first half term, Free time in the second and third, Local area and Social media after; Foirm is An freagra eile and Ceist; the pen writes fada! and mutation notes only; three questions on Gan do scríbhinn.
- Year 9: tier 2 on the same pages; Foirm adds Aimsir and Bearna; five questions.
- Year 10: tier 3 on the same pages, plus Culture and Extra-curricular; cad chuige? and bheith ag begin; the opener waits twenty seconds.
- Year 11: two pages from twelve beside page 1; Scrúdú, Bealtaine 2028 in the date cell; the countdown; the pace line above one line a night; Seol offered once a half term for a sheet with new drafts.
- Year 12: as Year 11 with Bealtaine 2027; twice a half term if the department asks.

## The exam-script register, what it must do

Every screen must pass as a page of a candidate's script, or as the card sent to the teacher. The pen writes only what a teacher could check; the card carries the question in print, the pupil's line, the ráite tick and its date in the pupil's biro where one exists, a tick box for the teacher, Ceart mar atá, a blank rule for the teacher's correction and Mo cheist at the foot.

## For a pupil with ADHD

Progress is an object that fills: a sheet, scríofa inside a fortnight, Críochnaithe inside the half term, its ráite column filling in biro one class at a time, seolta once, ceartaithe when the card comes back, whenever that is, with nothing waiting on it. The same four items in the same order every night, one live, the class tick one tap beneath item 1 and never a fifth thing; the queue never shown. Marking is instant and glossed; one wrong form is written out again before the night closes. Le ceartú holds five things and empties. No streak, no timer until Year 11. The date at the top is always a class day the pupil can count to.

## What comes out of the current build

The TabView; HomeView's statistics and streak; the score slot in ExamHeader; GrammarView; SettingsView's Form and stage picker; intervals 0, 1, 3, 7, 14, 30; per-word ticks; the Audio folder; Years 11 and 12 only; Ceist ar ais; Mo chuid botún; the printed-sheet gate; the twenty-second wait and grey pages below Year 10; the name Cuimhne.

## What has to be true before it can be built

Data: the topic table; the 270 questions with gloss, tier, exam-only flag, sameAs, register, pair and opener; the department's calendar of pages, the school's term dates and whether the timetable runs on one week or two; the school's name in Irish; the catchment townlands with locatives; the verb paradigm table; a gender supplement for Year 8 words; every Irish string signed off by the Principal. Audio: none until Abair. One booklet, Years 8 to 12, in iCloud; phonics and the conversation itself are the classroom's, in the Irish class the timetable already holds; the teacher's reading reaches the script only by the pupil entering it.
