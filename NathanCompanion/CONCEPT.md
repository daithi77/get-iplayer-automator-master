# What the app is, in plain words

Dáithí Murray, with Claude. 26 September 2026, evening. This replaces the long brief of the same day, which is kept for the record in data/CONCEPT-long-2026-09-26.md.

## In one sentence

A notebook in which a pupil writes their own answers to the questions an Irish speaker will ask them, one a night, until every answer is theirs.

## What the pupil sees

**A notebook.** It looks like a ruled copybook with a red margin, because that is what a pupil already writes Irish in. Each page is one topic (Mé féin agus mo theaghlach, An scoil, Caitheamh aimsire, and so on, twelve in all). Each line of a page is one of the 270 questions, printed in small type, with the pupil's own answer written under it in biro blue.

**Tonight.** Opening the app lands the pupil on tonight's page. It asks for two things and no more:

1. One old answer, written again from memory. The question is shown; the answer is hidden. The pupil types it. The red pen marks it in your handwriting: a tick, or fada!, or the missing séimhiú written above the word.
2. One new answer, to the next question on the page, in the pupil's own words. A short opener in grey helps them start (Tá mé i mo chónaí i …). The red pen marks what it can: fadas and mutations. It never marks what it cannot judge.

Then the pen writes the date and one kind line, and the night is over. Five minutes. Nothing else is asked, nothing else is shown.

**The notebook fills.** Over weeks the page fills with the pupil's answers. Old answers come back to be rewritten from memory at growing intervals: the next night, then two nights later, then four, then a week, then a fortnight. An answer that has been rewritten right from memory several times is left alone. One rewritten wrong comes back the next night. That is the whole learning method, and it is the one the new Key Stage 3 framework asks for.

**Your teacher.** Any page can be sent to the pupil's teacher as a PDF, ruled and marked exactly as it looks on screen, with a blank rule under each answer for the teacher's own pen. If the teacher corrects an answer, the pupil types the correction in, and that becomes the answer the app expects from then on.

## Who it is for

Every year from 8 to 12. A Year 8 answers the simple questions (name, age, family, where they live) in one sentence each. A Year 12 answers the full set, in full. The questions are marked by the year they first open, so a Year 8 never sees a question about GCSE options or work experience. Nothing on screen says what year a pupil is in beyond that.

For a pupil with ADHD it is the same app: two things a night, in the same order every night, marked at once, never a list of what is still to do.

## Two tools beside the notebook

They are there when a pupil wants them and are never forced on anyone.

**Focail.** Word practice: English shown, Irish typed, from the topic the pupil is writing about. Verbs ask for the root and the verbal noun. Spaced the same way as the answers.

**Gramadach.** A drill on one form at a time: the article with a noun, a preposition with the article, a verb in a tense. Generated from the word lists and from the sentences taken out of your grammar book, rewritten in Ulster Irish and made modern. A wrong answer is corrected in the margin, in your hand, with the rule in one line.

## Settings, kept short

Name and class. Year. Foirm tháite or foirm scartha for the present and future first person plural (cuirimid or cuireann muid); the pen accepts either whatever is set. Strict fadas on or off. Send the notebook to a teacher: their address.

## What is not in it

No streaks, no scores, no percentages, no timers. No dates on the cover, no candidate box devices for the younger years. No audio until the Abair Ulster voice is wired in; questions stay in print. No tab bar: the notebook is the app, and the two tools and the settings are reached from a single line at the foot of the page.

## What it is built from

The 270 questions, each with its English gloss, its year, its natural follow-up and its opener, drafted and awaiting your sign-off in the workbook. The Key Stage 3 word list for Years 8 to 10 and the GCSE list for Years 11 and 12, also in the workbook. The grammar sentences from your book, rewritten. Your handwriting as the pen.

## What comes out of the current build

The five tabs, the home page statistics and streak, the score in the header, the separate speaking tab (the notebook is the speaking preparation), the stage switch. The exam-script look stays.
